# GaSIO wins on fuel flexibility, not novelty

GaSIO (VCBG) has a real technology edge, but it is narrow, it rests mostly on our own claims, and it is not "novelty" in either the patent sense or the state-of-the-art sense. Continuous-feed gasification of loose rice husk has been prior art since Belonio's Philippine work in the 2000s. Chinese patents already cover household semi-gasifiers and gasifiers with feeders. "Volumetric" is a brand term that appears nowhere in the gasification literature outside CCS, Oxfam and donor documents. What GaSIO plausibly does better is combine things no major competitor sells together. The household stove claims ISO 19867 tier performance level with the best biomass stove on the market (Mimi Moto: Tier 5 efficiency and CO, Tier 4 PM2.5), while burning **unpelletised residues fed continuously**, co-producing biochar, at a **USD 25** unsubsidised price. The ≥75 kW tea-roaster retrofit unit has no commercial equivalent we could find in Vietnam, China, India, Sri Lanka or Kenya. Every one of these differentiators is unproven in public. The CREEC test report is not published, no peer-reviewed paper exists, no patent number could be verified in any database, and no installed-base data is public. Several headline numbers would fail a funder's first read: 83.7% household efficiency, 95% fixed carbon, 5.6 kg CO2 per kg biochar, CO2-per-stove figures that differ fivefold, and a 20 t/h boiler claim alongside a 3 MW ceiling. The market is large enough: a few hundred million USD of equipment value per Vietnamese segment, with boiler retrofits the largest pool. Carbon finance (cookstove avoidance credits plus biochar removal credits) can roughly double unit economics, but only with metered MRV. In industry terms, this document is a **Technology & Market Assessment**, structured as an **integrated commercial and technical due diligence**. Its novelty component is formally a **patentability/freedom-to-operate (FTO) search** or a **patent landscape report**.

## This document is a technology and market assessment in due-diligence form

No single industry label covers all four parts of this analysis: technology, market, competitors and novelty. When investors commission the work on a company, it is called **Commercial Due Diligence (CDD)** plus **Technical/Technology Due Diligence (TDD)**. The ICAEW publishes a formal CDD guideline covering market size and trajectory, competitive position and whether the growth case holds ([ICAEW](https://www.icaew.com/-/media/corporate/files/technical/corporate-finance/guidelines/icaew-commercial-due-diligence-guideline.ashx)). RICS publishes a TDD standard covering asset condition and fitness for use ([RICS](https://rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/standards/Technical_Due_Diligence_Of_Commercial_Property_1st_Edition.pdf)). L.E.K. argues that hardware investors need the two **integrated**, because technical findings feed the commercial thesis ([L.E.K.](https://www.lek.com/insights/technology/why-investors-need-integrated-commercial-and-technology-due-diligence)). Outside a transaction, the same content is usually sold as a **"Technology & Market Assessment"**, which is Lux Research's term for assessing market opportunity, best-positioned technologies and commercial readiness ([Lux Research](https://luxresearchinc.com/?p=2160)). Development funders such as GCF and UNIDO use **(pre-)feasibility study**, following the UNIDO *Manual for the Preparation of Industrial Feasibility Studies* ([UNIDO](https://downloads.unido.org/ot/47/90/4790657/00001-10000_07534.pdf); [GCF SAP Annex 2](https://www.greenclimate.fund/document/guidance-preparing-pre-feasibility-study-annex-2-under-sap)). Legal novelty is never settled in a market report. It belongs to a **patentability (novelty) search**, a defensive **FTO opinion**, or a **patent landscape report** under WIPO's guidelines ([Spruson & Ferguson](https://www.spruson.com/understanding-the-differences-between-patentability-novelty-and-freedom-to-operate-prior-art-searching/); [WIPO PUB 946](https://tind.wipo.int/record/28858/files/wipo_pub_946.pdf)). Because CCS is writing about itself, the most accurate cover title is **"GaSIO Technology & Market Assessment (vendor-prepared Technology and Commercial Due Diligence)"**. This report follows that structure. The EIC Accelerator expects TAM/SAM/SOM, competitor and USP comparison, SWOT and an FTO annex ([BayFOR](https://www.bayfor.org/fileadmin/user_upload/BayFOR-pdfs/aktuelles/veranstaltungen/BayFOR-eic-accelerator-full-application-public-natalia-garcia-mozo.pdf); [LT EIC info day](https://lino.lmt.lt/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/EIC-Info-day-LT-Project-selection-and-expectations-to-the-applicant_Vilma-Radvilaite_final.pdf)), so the same skeleton serves grant applications too.

## One gasifier core spread across eleven applications at uneven maturity

The product line is a single platform. VCBG is "volumetric continuous biomass gasification": oxygen-limited gasification that yields syngas, burned close-coupled for heat, and biochar. CCS claims it handles all local residues and runs uninterrupted because feed is continuous ([CCS materials](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/12kuOvWiRUNr9nZ9Ob0MuBLvGxovKEBae)). The 2025 Oxfam/CCS brochure lists the design advantages as continuous feed with "minimalist design", "maximum ratio of thermal power to equipment volume", a reaction chamber under negative pressure to prevent gas leaks, and low fuel-preparation requirements. It calls VCBG "the most advanced gasification technology version available today", but gives no reactor geometry, air path or char-discharge mechanism ([Oxfam/CCS VCBG brochure](https://oi-files-cng-v2-prod.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/vietnam.oxfam.org/s3fs-public/file_attachments/Brochure%20VCBG%20-%20final.pdf)). The inventor is MSc Nguyễn Hồng Long, director of CCS ([VnExpress](https://vnexpress.net/khoa-hoc-cong-nghe/cuoc-thi-sang-kien-khoa-hoc/san-pham/bep-dan-sinh-gasio-808); [Clean Cooking Alliance directory](https://cleancooking.org/sector-directory/center-for-creativity-and-sustainability-study-and-consultancy-ccs/)). Research began in August 2010 ([Công Thương](https://congthuong.vn/nhan-rong-cong-nghe-khi-hoa-sinh-khoi-trong-che-bien-nong-san-can-hoan-thien-chinh-sach-147845.html)). CCS's own 2016 R&D update describes five lab prototypes of a continuous-feed stove refuelling in 15-minute cycles ([CCS update](http://www.ccspin.org/index.php/en/news-information/303-update-on-continuous-gasifier-r-d-progress)).

## Novelty verdict: incremental invention with a positional performance edge

Asking whether GaSIO is "novel" mixes three questions with different answers. This follows the standard split between patentability, technical state of the art and KPI benchmarking ([WIPO PCT Art. 33](https://www.wipo.int/pct/en/texts/articles/a33.html)).

### Legal and patent novelty is unverified, territorial and probably narrow

The materials claim a 2023 Vietnamese invention patent ("method and device for biomass gasification"), a 2017 Vietnamese utility solution for the household stove, a 2020 Danish patent, and two pending Vietnamese applications ([CCS materials](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/12kuOvWiRUNr9nZ9Ob0MuBLvGxovKEBae)). The only independent reference says the furnace "has been registered for IP protection partially in Vietnam and Denmark", with no certificate numbers ([Công Thương](https://congthuong.vn/nhan-rong-cong-nghe-khi-hoa-sinh-khoi-trong-che-bien-nong-san-can-hoan-thien-chinh-sach-147845.html)). The UN CTC-N page mentions "a patent for a biomass gasification method and equipment", also without numbers ([CTC-N](https://www.ctc-n.org/solutions/volumetric-continuous-biomass-gasification-technology-vcbg-sustainable-solution-agri-food)). Searches by applicant name, by inventor and by the exact Vietnamese title returned only unrelated patents, such as another applicant's EP3088492B1 with an almost identical title ([EP3088492B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP3088492B1/en)). We found no PCT filing and no Danish company. The likely consequences are serious. A Vietnamese utility solution filed in 2017 expires around 2027 (10-year term). A Danish right, if it is a utility model ("brugsmodel"), was registered without substantive examination and protects nothing outside Denmark. With no PCT filed inside the 12-month Paris priority window, foreign protection for the original embodiment is probably lost, and CCS's own publications (the 2015 award, press, CTC-N, workshops) now count as prior art against any new filing on the same subject. These points are general knowledge of Vietnamese and Danish IP practice, not verified facts about CCS's files. Ownership is also unclear. A LinkedIn profile ties the inventor to "EBG Science and Technology Company Ltd.", which may be a commercial vehicle separate from the non-profit CCS ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/hong-long-nguyen-5b390615/)). An investor would ask who holds the rights before anything else. **Verdict: legal novelty cannot be asserted. At best it covers specific embodiments in Vietnam and possibly Denmark.**

### Technical state of the art: the concept is old, the combination is the only candidate

Continuous feeding of small-particle biomass is well documented. Belonio's household continuous-flow rice-husk gas stove "can be operated continuously until all cooking jobs is finished" ([bioenergylists](https://stoves.bioenergylists.org/node/2962)). His group published a continuous-type household rice-husk gasifier for Bangladesh in 2012 ([Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Title:-Design-of-a-continuous-%E2%80%94-Type-rice-husk-and-Belonio-Bhuiyan/5559a8e178ec6ffb62084aa99a66c2852b2c09d1)) and built a 19 kW continuous-flow gasifier for thermal applications ([bioenergylists](https://stoves.bioenergylists.org/node/2878)). GIZ's micro-gasification handbook describes natural-draft continuous-feed husk "semi-gasifiers" ([Energypedia/GIZ](https://energypedia.info/images/f/f6/Micro_Gasification_Cooking_with_gas_from_biomass.pdf)). Chinese patents cover a household biomass semi-gasifier (CN102538019B), a gasification furnace with a series-connected feeding device (CN105482853B) and a continuous pyrolysis-gasification furnace (CN101412915B) ([CN102538019B](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN102538019B/zh); [CN105482853B](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN105482853B/zh); [CN101412915B](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN101412915B/zh)). Chinese tobacco-curing literature reports "adding fuel without extinguishing" as an achieved goal by 2010 ([CASB 2010](https://www.casb.org.cn/CN/Y2010/V26/I14/392)). Vietnam has its own prior art: VINASILIC's 2010 "Magic Flame" TLUD pellet stove at USD 10–15 ([bioenergylists](https://stoves.bioenergylists.org/node/2618)) and Newtech's "Magic Fire" gasifier stoves with their own patent claims ([bioenergylists](https://stoves.bioenergylists.org/node/3222)). None of the following concepts is new: continuous feed, negative-pressure (fan-in-flue) reactors, syngas plus biochar, multi-feedstock capability, and a household-to-industrial range. Oxfam's own terms of reference expand VCBG as "**Vertical** Continuous Biomass Gasification" ([NGO Centre ToR](https://ngocentre.org.vn/vi/node/55482)), and the brochure's explanation of "volumetric" is a power-density ratio, not a distinct gasification regime. The one technical step that could matter is a design that keeps a TLUD-like clean pyrolytic front while refuelling continuously and extracting char without disturbing the reaction zone, in a low-part-count build. Historical continuous-feed husk stoves never managed that. Belonio's household version reached only about **21% efficiency** ([bioenergylists](https://stoves.bioenergylists.org/node/2962)), and Energypedia notes semi-gasifiers struggled with smoke during refuelling ([Energypedia](https://energypedia.info/wiki/Gasifier_Stoves)). **Verdict: GaSIO is an incremental, inventive-step-level improvement on known prior art. It is not a pioneering technology class, and "the most advanced gasification technology available today" cannot be defended.**

### Performance edge: credible at the top of the field, unique only in combination

The edge GaSIO can defend is positional: performance level with the best stoves, on fuels and at a price they cannot match. On ISO/TR 19867-3 tiers, the claimed profile (Tier 5 efficiency ≥50%, Tier 5 CO ≤3.0 g/MJd, Tier 4 PM2.5 ≤62 mg/MJd) matches Mimi Moto exactly ([CCA tier summary](https://cleancooking.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/626-1-1.pdf); [CCA Mimi Moto](https://cleancooking.org/innovations/clean-burning-gasification-stove/)). Tier 5 PM2.5 (≤5 mg/MJd) is in practice out of reach for any solid-biomass stove. Matching Mimi Moto would therefore put GaSIO in the top group, not above it. The difference is fuel. Mimi Moto and Inyenyeri depend on a pellet supply chain ([CCA profile](https://cleancooking.org/?p=11063); [E4C Inyenyeri](https://www.engineeringforchange.org/solutions/product/inyenyeri-fuel-pellets/)). ACE, BioLite and BURN are batch-fed with wood or charcoal. We found no major commercial competitor advertising continuous feeding of loose husk, sawdust or cobs at Tier 4–5. Continuous feeding could also address the main field weakness of batch gasifiers: refuelling causes the largest and most variable emission spikes, and Mimi Moto fell to roughly Tier 3–4 in Rwandan homes ([Atmosphere 2019](http://dx.doi.org/10.3390/atmos10050290)). Continuous top-up of loose residue could equally create its own spikes. That makes it the single most valuable test CCS can run. At industrial scale, GaSIO's claimed ~73–75% is below the 85–90% that Vietnamese fluidized-bed boiler makers market ([Kim Trường Phúc](https://kimtruongphuc.vn/lo-hoi-biomass); [Hoa Sen](https://lohoibiomass.vn/lo-hoi-dot-sinh-khoi-biomass-nhien-lieu-xanh-cho-tuong-lai-ben-vung/)). The industrial edge is therefore not efficiency. It is small size (≥75 kW, 55–65 kg), retrofit of existing roasters and boilers, tolerance of minimally processed residues, and close-coupled syngas combustion that avoids the tar and gas-cleaning problems that sink small power gasifiers ([MDPI Energies 2021](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/14/20/6711)). **Verdict: the performance edge is real but narrow, and conditional on publishing third-party data obtained on loose residues during continuous refuelling.**

At USD 25, GaSIO undercuts every unsubsidised forced-draft competitor: Mimi Moto, BioLite at USD 60 and ACE at USD 140–165. It costs three to four times a basic Vietnamese improved wood stove and sits in the same band as carbon-subsidised African retail, where a USD 40 efficient stove can sell for USD 5 ([AP via ABC](https://abcnews.com/Business/wireStory/carbon-credit-financing-emerges-africas-biggest-bet-expanding-136247673)). That strength only holds if USD 25 covers the fan and power supply. We could not determine whether the household fan runs on mains or a battery, and that changes the comparison with ACE's solar-battery stove. The competitors' real advantages are not technical. They have scale (68,000 to 5.2 million units), registered carbon projects with metered monitoring, PAYGo and fuel-subscription finance, and **public, catalog-listed ISO data**. Companies with carbon programmes captured **95% of all 2024 clean-cooking investment** ([CCA](https://cleancooking.org/news/latest-clean-cooking-industry-snapshot-identifies-trends-in-investment-revenue-and-carbon-markets/)). This is the gap GaSIO has to close. In the camping segment, the claimed 2-minute heat-up would be roughly twice as fast as BioLite's 4.5 minutes per litre. But weight is not stated, there is no brand or retail distribution, and military or humanitarian procurement would require testing CCS does not have.

The industrial picture reverses the household one. GaSIO is neither the cheapest nor the most efficient. Vietnamese boiler makers sell certified 1 t/h units at about USD 9–20 per kWth, and Chinese husk burners list at USD 14–23 per kW. Any retrofit burner GaSIO sells has to price well under the cost of a whole new local boiler. The defensible ground is where those rivals do not go. It starts with **small direct-heat retrofits**: a 55–65 kg unit bolted onto an existing tea roaster by widening the firebox door, which no other commercial product we found does. Then comes **unprocessed residue** in place of the dried, sized fuel (below ~20% moisture) that downdraft and European CHP gasifiers need ([Assam study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/280769582_Possibility_of_biomass_gasification_in_tea_manufacturing_industries_in_Assam_India)). Last is **biochar at distributed scale**. The tea baseline is mostly firewood. Kenyan and Indonesian benchmarks put wood fuel at roughly USD 0.10 per kg made tea ([Carbon Trust](https://tea.carbontrust.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/BSEAA2_Bioenergy-in-tea-processing-sector-report.pdf); [UGM](https://ir.lib.ugm.ac.id/id/eprint/20407/)), so a 20% saving against firewood is worth only one to two US cents per kilogram. The sales argument against firewood is labour, smoke-free quality and throughput. The 50–80% fuel-saving claims only bite against coal, LPG and electricity.

Heat pumps are the medium-term threat. At COP 3–5 they deliver heat at roughly 140–230 VND/MJ. Husk at the claimed 73% efficiency costs about 150 VND/MJ, against roughly 500 VND/MJ for LPG and 690 VND/MJ for resistive electricity. These are derived from dated vendor fuel prices of LPG 16,000–23,000 VND/kg, electricity 2,000–3,000 VND/kWh and husk ~1,500 VND/kg ([thietbiaplucbachkhoa](https://thietbiaplucbachkhoa.vn/so-sanh-chi-phi-nhien-lieu-co-ban-cho-lo-hoi)). Heat pumps cannot reach the 300–350 °C of kill-green or pan-roasting ([Food Science 2017](https://www.spkx.net.cn/CN/Y2017/V38/I9/126)). Our product line is therefore safest in high-temperature roasting, steam, distillation and boiling, and most exposed in low-temperature drying floors and the hybrid solar dryer.

## Market sizing: a few hundred million USD per Vietnamese segment, boilers largest

Demand is not the binding constraint. About **2.1 billion people** worldwide still relied on polluting cooking fuels in 2023 ([Tracking SDG7 2025](https://www.irena.org/Publications/2025/Jun/Tracking-SDG-7-The-Energy-Progress-Report-2025)), including **120 million in Southeast Asia**, almost one-fifth of the region ([IEA SEAO 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/southeast-asia-energy-outlook-2026/executive-summary)). In Vietnam, about **22% of people still use traditional Tier 0–1 biomass stoves**, though the data dates from about 2021 ([SEforALL](https://www.seforall.org/system/files/2021-10/TTP-Vietnam-Brief.pdf)). Industrial heat is the bigger prize. Vietnam has about **9,000 industrial boilers**, mostly in textiles and footwear. About 30% already burn biomass, and an official estimate says **over 6,000 could be retrofitted or replaced** ([NGO Environment](https://ngoenvironment.com/en/reducing-emissions-through-industrial-boiler-conversion.html)). Youngone's Nam Định factory spent USD 2.7 million replacing three coal boilers with husk-pellet units ([VietnamNet](https://vietnamnet.vn/en/nam-dinh-textile-factory-eliminates-coal-fired-boilers-shifts-to-biomass-2375933.html)). Tea is the proven beach-head: about **128,000 ha** planted, about USD 238 million of exports in 2025 ([MAE](https://en.mae.gov.vn/quality-processing-and-market-development/vietnamese-tea-industry-establishes-a-new-global-standing-9183.htm)), and about **67,500 tea households** in Thái Nguyên alone ([Vietnam.vn](https://www.vietnam.vn/en/mo-rong-thi-truong-tieu-thu-bang-san-xuat-sach)). Feedstock is abundant. Credible totals are 79 Mt of crop residues (MOIT 2017) to 95–98 Mt (IAE 2019), including ~9 Mt of rice husk ([FFTC-AP](https://ap.fftc.org.tw/article/2786)), and about 80% of rice straw is burned in the field ([IIASA](https://iiasa.ac.at/web/home/research/researchPrograms/air/8_Rice_burning_October_2018-Nguyen_Hong_Nam.pdf)). One caution: Vietnam exported **6.03 Mt of wood pellets** in 2024 at about USD 134/t ([VnEconomy](https://en.vneconomy.vn/vietnams-wood-pellet-industry-has-plenty-of-room-to-grow.htm)). That pulls clean residues toward export parity and favours a gasifier that runs on low-grade husk, prunings and bark over pellet-dependent rivals.

Three things follow from the sizing. First, **boiler retrofits are the largest Vietnamese pool, but GaSIO is least proven there**. Today's TRL 4–5 for large boilers is the gap between our biggest market and our evidence. Second, **the household SOM is small in equipment value** (single-digit millions of USD) and only becomes a business with carbon revenue and a distribution partner. INTRACO/Carbon Viet Nam's Verra programme already covers 850,000 stoves across 30 provinces ([Carbon Viet Nam](https://carbonvietnam.com/taxonomy-project/improved-cookstove-projects/)), so it is both a potential channel and an incumbent. Third, **policy helps power, not heat**. Revised PDP8 targets 1,523–2,699 MW of biomass power by 2030 ([Watson Farley & Williams](https://www.wfw.com/articles/vietnam-makes-major-updates-to-power-development-plan-viii/)), JETP's USD 15.5 billion is mostly grid and power ([Climate Action Tracker](https://climateactiontracker.org/countries/vietnam/)), and the pilot ETS covers only thermal power, steel and cement ([ICAP](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/vietnam-issues-rules-pilot-ets-launching-august-2025)). Clean-cooking money is also skewed away from Asia: the USD 2.2 billion Paris summit pledges are Africa-focused ([IEA](https://www.iea.org/news/landmark-summit-mobilises-22-billion-to-make-2024-a-turning-point-for-clean-cooking-access-in-africa)). Tracked enterprise finance totals only about USD 196 million a year against an USD 8 billion annual need ([CCA](https://cleancooking.org/)).

## Carbon finance can double unit economics, but only with metered MRV

**Cookstove avoidance credits have split into two tiers, and only the premium tier is worth entering.** Legacy cookstove credits averaged about **USD 4/t** in 2026, while CORSIA-eligible credits traded above **USD 14/t** ([MSCI](https://www.msci.com/research-and-insights/quick-take/clean-cooking-dominates-corsia-but-premium-faces-pressure)). CCP-labelled Sub-Saharan Africa cookstove credits were USD 15 in May 2026 ([Fastmarkets](https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/cookstove-developers-face-ccp-vs-corsia-trade-off-as-price-premiums-clash/)). The cause is the integrity reset. Berkeley researchers found **9.2x over-crediting** across cookstove methodologies ([Berkeley News](https://news.berkeley.edu/2024/01/23/as-carbon-offsets-cookstove-emission-credits-are-greatly-overestimated)). In March 2025 the ICVCM approved only Gold Standard Metered & Measured, GS TPDDTEC v4 and Verra VM0050, all with strict fNRB and fuel-measurement conditions ([Carbon Herald](https://carbonherald.com/icvcm-gives-green-light-for-three-stricter-cookstove-methodologies/)). Verra's older VMR0006 is replaced by VM0050, mandatory from the 2027 vintage ([Verra](https://verra.org/methodologies/vmr0006-methodology-for-installation-of-high-efficiency-firewood-cookstoves/)). For Vietnam, the decisive variable is fNRB. Project-reported fNRB has been **67%**, against a MoFuSS estimate of about 34% and a CDM default of 0.3 ([BeZero](https://bezerocarbon.com/insights/why-fnrb-is-critical-in-our-assessment-of-household-devices-projects); [MoFuSS](https://www.mofuss.unam.mx/mofuss/files/Report_on_Updated_fNRB_Values_20_June_2024.pdf)). That roughly halves credits per stove compared with legacy Vietnamese projects. Our 3–5 t CO2 per stove per year flyer claim (company claim) sits at the top of the market range: Mimi Moto 4–5 t, ACE 2–3 t, and a planning range of 2.5–3.5 t for gasifier/TLUD stoves ([CCA](https://cleancooking.org/?p=11063); [Carbon Workbench](https://thecarbonworkbench.com/cookstove-calculator)). Under VM0050 or GS Metered with conservative fNRB, an assumed 1–3 tCO2e per stove per year at USD 10–15 gives about **USD 10–45 per stove per year**. That is enough to subsidise a USD 25 stove within one to three years, provided usage is metered with temperature or fuel sensors, which adds hardware cost.

**Biochar removal credits are GaSIO's unique stacking angle.** Biochar is the volume leader in durable carbon removal. About **3.04 Mt** was contracted from 2022 to H1 2025, and market value grew from USD 14.6 million (2022) to USD 181.5 million (2024) ([CDR.fyi](https://www.cdr.fyi/blog/biochar-carbon-removal-market-snapshot-2025)). Puro's CORCCHAR index was **€120–130/t** in 2026 ([Puro.earth](https://puro.earth/carbon-removal-index-price); [Senken](https://www.senken.io/reports/carbon-removal-prices-2026-2040-en.pdf)), and artisanal Indian biochar about **€105/t** ([Senken academy](https://www.senken.io/academy/biochar)). Microsoft has signed Asian distributed-reactor deals, including more than 100,000 t from Varaha's 18 reactors in India ([AgFunderNews](https://agfundernews.com/varaha-signs-major-offtake-agreement-with-microsoft-for-biochar-carbon-removal-in-india)). Standards for distributed production exist. CSI Global Artisan C-Sink defines a "**C-Sink Cook**" as a family using char-producing stoves, grouped in villages of more than 50 families ([CSI](https://www.carbon-standards.com/en/standards/service-505~global-artisan-c-sink.html)). That definition was written for TLUDs but maps directly onto GaSIO. Verra VM0044 v1.2 was CCP-approved in 2025 ([Verra](https://verra.org/verra-publishes-minor-revision-to-biochar-methodology/)). The removal arithmetic is less generous than our materials imply. Assume, pending company data, 100–180 kg of char per household per year and 1.5–2.5 tCO2e net per tonne of char. That gives about 0.15–0.45 tCO2e per household, or **€15–45 per household per year**, comparable to the avoidance credit. A tea-roasting unit burning 11 t of biomass a year (company claim), at a 20–35% char yield (a general literature range, not a GaSIO measurement), would produce roughly 2–4 t of char. That is worth about USD 600–1,100 in domestic product sales plus 3–10 tCO2e of removals. A 1,500 t/yr industrial tea line would produce 300–500 t of char, close to the 500–600 t/yr scale that credit developers treat as stand-alone viable ([Planboo](https://planboo.eco/requirements-to-earn-carbon-credits-through-biochar/)). One reality check comes from the region's leading peer. HUSK in Cambodia earns only **10–20% of revenue from credits** and the rest from fertilizer ([Nitidae 2025](https://www.nitidae.org/files/00f06c3a/biochar_market_study_nitidae_2025_vf.pdf)), so the agronomic product matters more than the credit. Double-claiming rules between avoidance and removal credits on the same stove are unverified and must be cleared with a standard body before marketing a "stacked" model.

**Vietnamese law now permits this but adds friction.** Decree 112/2026 (effective 19 May 2026) requires MAE approval for transfers with a corresponding adjustment. It caps export at 90% for "category 1" activities and 50% for others, and its annex names "biogas and biochar from rice straw and agricultural by-products" ([VILAF](https://www.vilaf.com.vn/blog/defining-carbon-value-vietnam-introduces-rules-for-international-transfers-under-decree-112/); [The Investor](https://theinvestor.vn/vietnam-opens-door-to-international-carbon-credit-trading-d18764.html)). We could not confirm which cap applies to cookstoves and biochar. The Singapore Article 6 agreement was approved in August 2026, and the Japan JCM was upgraded to Article 6 in May 2026 ([CarbonCredits.com](https://carboncredits.com/vietnam-approves-singapore-carbon-pact-article-6/); [green.earth](https://www.green.earth/news/japan-and-vietnam-upgrade-jcm-to-meet-article-6-rules)). JCM is a natural fit for boiler retrofits in Japanese-owned or Japan-supplying factories. Domestic ETS offset demand (up to 30% of obligations) only becomes real once domestic methodologies are published and trading is live. Organisations cannot propose methodologies until 1 January 2028 ([Vinacontrol](https://vinacontrol.com.vn/news/decoding-decree-1192025nd-cp-the-key-to-entering-vietnams-carbon-market)).

## Sixteen claims that undermine credibility, and how to fix them

These are the numbers a funder, carbon auditor or CCA reviewer will check first. Each is a company claim from the CCS materials or the Oxfam/CCS brochure unless noted. Most fixes are low-cost, and the credibility gain is large. A later, fuller line-by-line check of all eight PDFs (33 claims, with the calculations and a question list for CCS) is kept in a separate fact-check document. It adds four findings not in this table:
- "Over 1.7 million t CO2/yr" from Vietnamese fossil fuels is about 200x too low.
- The green-tea unit's CO2 is given as about 1.5 t lifetime in the overview but 12 t/yr in its flyer.
- The tea unit's tested durability (25.2 months minimum) conflicts with its claimed 10-year life.
- IPCC defaults put rice-husk gasifier char at only about 0.3–0.5 kg CO2 stored per kg.

Two other figures checked out and are worth keeping. "2,800 t CO2/yr for a 1,000 t/yr coal-fired black-tea line" is consistent with 0.6–1.2 kg coal per kg dried black tea ([CIGR review](https://cigrjournal.org/index.php/Ejounral/article/download/5048/2902/22934)): at a typical ~2.4–2.6 t CO2 per t coal (an engineering assumption), that implies 1,500–3,000 t CO2. "Boils 1 L in 2 min" implies about 2.6 kW of net heat to water, plausible at the top of a 1–6 kW range. The 12 t CO2/yr claim per tea unit, on 11 t of biomass, needs its baseline spelled out, because displacing partly renewable firewood earns far less than displacing coal or LPG.

## Conclusion

The question was whether GaSIO is novel and technically superior. The more useful answer is that GaSIO competes in a category where novelty is cheap and evidence is expensive. Belonio, Chinese utility-model holders and a dozen African stove companies have shown that continuous feeding, fan gasification and char-making stoves are all buildable. What none has done is ship them together, Tier 4–5 clean, on loose residues, for USD 25, with a retrofit version for tea roasters. That combination is our edge. It is defended less by patents, which are probably narrow and Vietnam-bound, than by speed to published data, know-how and a carbon-finance channel that rivals cannot copy without the char co-product. The danger is that the current materials spend this advantage. A reviewer who sees 83.7% efficiency or 5.6 kg CO2 per kg of char will discount the genuine Tier 5 claim along with them.

The commercial implication is that GaSIO's future value lies in proof, not in more product lines. One published ISO report on husk with refuelling data, one EBC char certificate, one QCVN-tested boiler retrofit and one metered carbon pilot would move the core products from "donor-backed technology" to "investable clean-heat platform". That is exactly the threshold where carbon-programme companies are now capturing nearly all sector investment. Vietnam's 2026 carbon legal framework and Microsoft-scale demand for Asian biochar have opened this window. Incumbent cookstove programmes and EcocharVietnam are positioned to fill it if CCS does not.

### C1 — Household stove: 1,027 kg/yr & 8 t lifetime (Overview) vs 3–5 t/yr & 30–50 t lifetime (Household flyer)
**Quoted (VN):** "Ước tính 1.027 kg CO2 được giảm thiểu mỗi năm nếu sử dụng bếp khí hóa" — Overview p.2; "Giảm khoảng 8 tấn CO2 trong vòng đời sản phẩm" — Overview p.3; "3 - 5 tấn/bếp/năm; và 30 - 50 tấn/bếp/vòng đời (10 năm)" — Household flyer
**Prior verdict:** Contradicted (5× gap; 1.027×10 = 10.3 ≠ 8)
**Loop A (direct):** `Vietnam improved cookstove project PDD firewood consumption per household kg per year baseline survey` → no Vietnamese PDD with a per-household figure; methodology defaults 0.5 t/capita/yr (VMR0006, GS Simplified) (L2). `Verra registry "Viet Nam" cookstove program ... VCS 2923 OR 3836 INTRACO` → VCS 2923 = 850,000 ICS in 30 provinces, 2022–2032 (L6 developer page). `Carbon Viet Nam VCS 2923 ... AMS-II.G baseline survey firewood` → Verra registry pages exist (grouped program Jan 2023; Phase 2 May 2024; KCM CPAs Yen Bai/Hoa Binh/Phu Tho) but PDD text not reachable. `"Viet Nam Improved Cookstove Project" KCM CDM PoA baseline wood ...` → PoA 10477, AMS-II.G v11, CPA-level totals only (L4). Searches for CCS/GaSIO's own basis for 1,027 kg: none of the CCS pages surfaced a calculation. Oxfam VCBG tag page: fetch failed.
**Loop B (benchmark):** `Gold Standard Vietnam cookstove project emission reductions per stove per year` → GS: typical 2.4–3.2 tCO2e/stove/yr (L5); Carbon Viet Nam annual totals 1.78 M (VCS 2923) and 265,486 tCO2e/yr (VCS 3836) → ≈2.1 t/stove/yr derived (L5/L6). `Mimi Moto "tonnes of CO2" per stove` → 4–5 t/yr, company claim, no independent verification (L6). `African Clean Energy ACE One "tonnes" CO2` → 2–5 t/yr (FAQ), 2–3 t/yr (Lesotho project), modelled (L6). `CDM TOOL30 default fNRB values Vietnam` → generic 0.3; draft A6.4 tool Vietnam 36 % (L2 draft). `Gill-Wiehl ... DOI` → 10.1038/s41893-023-01259-6, 9.2× over-crediting (L3). Arithmetic grid (L1), ER = wood × 1.747 × fNRB × displacement:

| Baseline wood (t/hh/yr) | fNRB 0.30, full | fNRB 0.36, full | fNRB 0.67, full | fNRB 1.0, full |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.8 (0.5 t/cap × 3.6) | 0.94 | 1.13 | 2.11 | 3.14 |
| 2.0 | 1.05 | 1.26 | 2.34 | 3.49 |
| 3.0 | 1.57 | 1.89 | 3.51 | 5.24 |

Halve these for 50 % displacement (stove stacking). **1,027 kg/yr ⇔ 1.96 t wood/yr × fNRB 0.30 × 1.747** (full displacement) — i.e., it reproduces a TOOL30-default-style calculation almost exactly (0.5 t/capita × ~3.9 persons). **3–5 t/yr requires** 4.8–7.9 t wood/yr at fNRB 0.36, or 2.6–4.3 t at fNRB 0.67, or 1.7–2.9 t at fNRB 1.0 with 100 % displacement — i.e., it needs either an implausibly large household baseline or fNRB ≈ 1 and zero stacking.
**Loop C (adversarial):** (i) Could 8 t lifetime be consistent with 1,027 kg/yr? Yes if a usage drop-off is applied: 8/1.027 = 7.8 effective stove-years over 10 years (≈78 % average usage), which is how methodologies treat adoption/drop-off — so the overview pair (1.027 t/yr; ~8 t) is internally defensible. (ii) Could 3–5 t/yr be true under a coal or LPG baseline? 3–5 tCO2 ⇔ 1.2–2.0 t coal or 1.0–1.7 t LPG per household-year — far above Vietnamese household use (an LPG household uses ~0.1–0.2 t/yr); so no. (iii) Could it be true if the biochar sink is added? Household char of ~100–180 kg/yr at ≤1.7 tCO2/t (Group A) adds ≤0.3 t/yr; still not 3–5. (iv) Competitor marketing (Mimi Moto 4–5 t, ACE 2–5 t) shows the 3–5 t figure is "industry-normal marketing", but Gill-Wiehl et al. (L3) show such figures are over-credited ~9× on average; the CCP conditions (fNRB ≤0.3 or MoFuSS) would cut Vietnamese legacy per-stove ERs (~2.1 t) to ~1.1 t. Search for a VHLSS/Ha Tinh per-household fuelwood figure: NOT RUN (budget).
**Best evidence level:** L1 arithmetic on L2 defaults (fNRB 0.30/0.36; 0.5 t/capita) plus L3 critique.
**New verdict:** **Contradicted** (internal) — refined: the Overview's 1,027 kg/yr is the methodology-consistent figure (matches a TOOL30-default calculation and a CCP-style estimate of ~1.1 t/stove/yr for Vietnam); the flyer's 3–5 t/yr is **Overstated ≈3–5×** against a CCP-compatible basis and ≈1.4–2.4× against even legacy Vietnamese program averages (~2.1 t/stove/yr). The 8 t vs 10.3 t lifetime gap is explainable by a usage drop-off and is not a real contradiction.
**Confidence:** 0.8 — the arithmetic and defaults are solid; the uncertainty is the Vietnam household baseline (no survey figure found) and CCS's own unknown basis.
**Change vs prior:** refined — prior "5× gap" retained for the flyer; the "1.027×10 ≠ 8" point is downgraded to a non-issue (usage decay reading).
**What CCS could send to settle it:** the ER calculation sheet behind 1,027 kg (baseline fuel kg/yr, fNRB, EF, usage/drop-off), the source of 3–5 t/yr, and any KPT/CCT or metered fuel data from the 181-household Thanh Hoa/Thai Nguyen pilot.
**Sources:** [CDM TOOL30 v3.0](https://cdm.unfccc.int/methodologies/PAmethodologies/tools/am-tool-30-v3.0.pdf); [A6.4 draft fNRB tool](https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/resource/A6.4-MEP011-A04.pdf); [MoFuSS 2024](https://www.mofuss.unam.mx/mofuss/files/Report_on_Updated_fNRB_Values_20_June_2024.pdf); [VMR0006 v1.1](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/imported/methodologies/VMR0006-Methodology-for-Installation-of-High-Efficiency-Firewood-Cookstoves-v1.1.pdf); [GS Simplified ICS](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/standards/408_V1.0_TC_EE_ICS_Simplified-Methodology-for-Efficient-Cookstoves.pdf); [Gold Standard response](https://www.goldstandard.org/news/gold-standards-response-to-allegations-of-over-crediting-clean-cooking); [Carbon Viet Nam VCS 2923](https://carbonvietnam.com/project/grouped-projects-for-viet-nam-cookstove-program-2923/); [PoA 10477](https://cdm.unfccc.int/ProgrammeOfActivities/poa_db/10XJ2BA7YRM9Z865K3EVDNPQGCTSW4/viewCPAs); [Gill-Wiehl et al. 2024 (RePEc)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natsus/v7y2024i2d10.1038_s41893-023-01259-6.html); [eScholarship OA](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/9650k3g9); [CCA Mimi Moto](https://cleancooking.org/?p=11063); [ACE FAQ](https://africancleanenergy.com/ace-faqs); [Berkeley SB008 input](https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/resource/SB008_call_for_input_annotations_Berkeley%20Carbon%20Trading%20Project_Cookstove.pdf)

### C2 — Service stove: 4.5–7.5 tCO2/stove/yr

A 1–20 kW stove run 6–8 h/day at ~10 kW burns roughly 5–8 t biomass/yr, so a small eatery/processor baseline of 3–6 t wood or 1.5–3 t coal/LPG is physically reasonable. **Best evidence level:** L1 arithmetic on L2 defaults.
**New verdict:** **Plausible** only if the baseline is coal/LPG or a heavy commercial firewood user (>7 t/yr); **Overstated ≈2–3×** for a typical firewood-using service kitchen at fNRB 0.36. Recorded as **Plausible (conditional)**.

**Change vs prior:** refined — from "Needs proof" to conditional plausibility with explicit baseline requirements.

### C7 — Camping stove: 720 g CO2 saved per meal; rechargeable battery; "unlimited" continuous cooking
**Quoted (VN):** "720 gram CO2"; "Hoạt động bằng pin, có thể sạc lại"; "Thời gian nấu liên tục: Không giới hạn" — Camping flyer

**Loop B (benchmark):** Stoichiometry (L1): butane 3.03 kgCO2/kg → a 230 g canister = 0.70 kg CO2; **240 g × 3.0 = 720 g**, i.e., the claim equals burning one full mini-canister (Vietnamese "bình gas mini" 220–250 g) per meal. Typical canister use (L6 gear sources): 13–19 g gas per litre boiled; ~50 g for a 20-min cook; 50–100 g per person-day → 45–300 g CO2 per typical camping meal. A 720 g figure therefore overstates a typical boil-and-cook meal by ~2.5–15×, but matches a long hot-pot (lẩu) meal that empties a mini canister. Wood baseline: 720 g CO2 ⇔ 412 g wood at fNRB 1 (a realistic 3-stone-fire meal uses 0.5–1.5 kg wood), or 1.1 kg wood at fNRB 0.36.
**Loop C (adversarial):** (i) Under a Vietnamese mini-canister hot-pot baseline the 720 g is exact — this is probably CCS's basis. (ii) The claim treats GaSIO's biomass CO2 as zero (renewable), which is standard practice. (iii) "Không giới hạn" continuous cooking: a battery-fan stove without a thermoelectric generator is limited by battery capacity — 3,200 mAh × 3.7 V ≈ 11.8 Wh → ~6–12 h at a 1–2 W fan; BioLite achieves long run times by a TEG that recharges while burning. The GaSIO flyer does not claim a TEG, so "unlimited" is only true while externally powered/recharged. (iv) Fuel feeding, not battery, is the practical limit for a continuous-feed stove — the wording may mean "no batch limit" rather than "no battery limit".
**Best evidence level:** L1 stoichiometry; L6 for stove specs and fuel-per-meal.
**New verdict:** **Plausible (conditional) / Overstated for typical use** — 720 g = one full 240 g butane mini-canister per meal; typical camping meals emit 45–300 g. "Rechargeable battery" Plausible (industry norm). "Unlimited continuous cooking" **Risky wording** — contradicted by battery physics unless a TEG or external power is present.
**Confidence:** 0.6 — stoichiometry is certain; the baseline-meal assumption is inferred, not sourced from CCS.
**Change vs prior:** refined — from "Needs proof" to a conditional reading with an identified likely basis.
**What CCS could send to settle it:** the meal/baseline definition behind 720 g; battery Wh, fan W and measured run time per charge; whether a TEG is fitted.

### A1 — "1 kg biochar buries 5.6 kg CO₂"
**Quoted (VN):** "1 kg than sinh học giúp chôn xuống đất 5,6kg cacbonic" — Service-stove flyer (Google Drive 1LxRc9KjiQvQqN6hok4ZQ5Hcu1v22OjUv), single page

**Loop A (direct):**

- `ccs.org.vn than sinh học bếp khí hóa GaSIO cacbon` → no ccs.org.vn page indexed; SWITCH-Asia BEST VCBG user manual (Oxfam + CCS, L6) says the stove is "carbon-negative because the biochar buries carbon in soil" — no quantity. Oxfam/BEST ToR (ngocentre.org.vn), baolaocai.vn: no figure.

- **Result:** CCS has never published the derivation of 5.6 anywhere indexed. The figure exists only in the service-stove flyer. Best source L6.
**Loop B (benchmark):**
- Stoichiometry (L1): 5.6 kg CO₂ ÷ 3.664 = **1.528 kg C per kg char = 153 % carbon**. Physically impossible for stored carbon.
- Every storage methodology found caps far below 5.6: IPCC 2019 Refinement Vol.4 Ch.2 App.4 Eq. 4Ap.1 = mass_dry × Fc × Fperm × 44/12 (L2, search extract via Verra VM0044 v1.0/v1.1 reproduction + Vermont legislature copy + naro.go.jp Japanese summary) → max for wood pyrolysis >600 °C = 0.77 × 0.89 × 3.664 = **2.51 kg CO₂/kg**. EBC C-sink certificates (L4, search extract): Interholco batch ba-cg-244-1-2 (bio.inspecta, 19 Sep 2023) 86.4 % C → **3.17 t CO₂e/t** biochar; CERES-CERT batch ba000054 62.8 % C → 2.305 t CO₂e/t. Puro.earth CORC audit statements list removal factors such as **2.517 tCO₂e per t biochar** (L5, search extract). Global Artisan C-Sink (CSI, L5): counts only 75 % of carbon as permanent and deducts 20 kg CO₂e/t safety margin + 30 kg CH₄/t → ≈2.4 kg CO₂e/kg at 86 % C.
- Cookstove-specific: Whitman et al. 2011 Kenya systems model (CCAFS sample, L3 search extract): pyrolytic stoves 3.69–4.33 tCO₂e/household/yr of which biochar is only 26–42 %; Gold Standard says cookstove projects typically credit 2.4–3.2 tCO₂e/stove/yr (fuel savings, not biochar).
**Loop C (adversarial):**
- `"kg CO2" per "kg biochar" 5.6 OR "5,6" avoided emissions combined storage cookstove` → **no source reports 5.6 kg CO₂ per kg biochar.** Closest route: Thunder Said Energy (L6) "converting each kg of dry biomass into biochar avoids 1.4 kg CO₂"; Environmental Research Letters 2024 (L3, LBL) best-practice cradle-to-gate CDR median **1.4 kg CO₂e per kg biomass** (poor practice: net +0.09). At a 25 % char yield, 1.4 kg CO₂e/kg biomass ÷ 0.25 = **5.6 kg CO₂e per kg char** — an arithmetical coincidence worth testing: CCS may have re-expressed a per-kg-*biomass* lifecycle figure (which bundles avoided fossil/biomass-burning emissions with the stored carbon) per kg of *char*. That would be a combined "avoided + stored" lifecycle metric, not "chôn xuống đất" (buried). No methodology (IPCC, EBC, Puro, Verra VM0044, Global Artisan C-Sink, Gold Standard) reports storage this way.
- Typo hypothesis: "5,6" ← "2,6"? 2.6 kg CO₂/kg = 71 % C, a realistic woody-char value. Unconfirmable without CCS.
- CO₂-equivalent hypothesis (CH₄/N₂O avoided): the Global Artisan C-Sink and EBC rules *deduct* methane rather than add it; soil N₂O suppression credits are not in any Tier-1 method. No route found to >3.66 via GWP factors for *stored* carbon.
- `"C-Sink Cook" OR "Cook C-Sink"` → **null**; CSI has only the Global Artisan C-Sink (Kon-Tiki) guideline indexed; no cookstove-specific C-sink rate found.
**Best evidence level:** L1
**New verdict:** Impossible (as a carbon-storage statement). Plausible only if re-labelled as a *lifecycle avoided-plus-stored* figure per kg char derived from ~1.4 kg CO₂e/kg biomass ÷ ~25 % yield — and even then "chôn xuống đất" is the wrong verb.
**Confidence:** 0.97 — the stoichiometric ceiling is arithmetic; the only reading that rescues 5.6 changes what the sentence says.
**Change vs prior:** refined — same verdict; added the one plausible origin (1.4 kg CO₂e/kg biomass ÷ 25 % yield) and documented that no standard reports storage above 3.66.
**What CCS could send to settle it:** the spreadsheet or report behind "5,6 kg"; whether it is per kg char or per kg feedstock, and which emissions it bundles.

### A2 — "1 kg biochar buries 3.5 kg CO₂"
**Quoted (VN):** "1kg than sinh học giúp chôn xuống đất 3,5kg cacbonic" — Household flyer (Drive 1aJQ93N61-P_Us646Dm1sPjFyy8NfbsqW) and Overview "2. Giới thiệu GaSIO", p.3
**Prior verdict:** Overstated (0.95 × 3.664 = 3.48 theoretical gross ceiling; IPCC defaults for gasification char Fc rice husk/straw 0.14, wood 0.52; Fperm 0.65/0.80/0.89 → 0.33–1.70 kg CO₂/kg)
**Loop A (direct):** Same CCS sources as A1 (World Bioenergy news, CTC-N page, SWITCH-Asia manual, Oxfam ToR) — none states 3.5 or its derivation. `"1 kg than sinh học" "3,5kg" cacbonic` → **null**. Vietnamese `"than sinh học" "1 kg" "kg CO2" chôn lấp tương đương` was queued but refused (budget). Best source L6.
**Loop B (benchmark):**
- **IPCC 2019 Refinement Table 4Ap.1 (Fc, organic C fraction of biochar) — search extracts from the Verra VM0044 v1.0/v1.1 reproduction ("Table 4: Values for Organic Carbon Content in Biochar from Table 4AP.1 of IPCC (2019)") and the Vermont copy:** Animal manure 0.38 ±49 % (pyro) / 0.09 ±53 % (gasif); **Wood 0.77 ±42 % / 0.52 ±52 %**; Herbaceous 0.65 ±45 % / 0.28 ±50 %; **Rice husks and rice straw 0.49 / 0.13 ±50 %**; Nut shells, pits and stones 0.74 / 0.40; Biosolids 0.35 / 0.07. **Correction to prior note: rice-husk gasification Fc is 0.13, not 0.14.**
- **Table 4Ap.2 (Fperm, fraction remaining after 100 yr):** "High temperature pyrolysis and gasification (>600 °C) 0.89 ±13 %; Medium temperature pyrolysis (450–600 °C) 0.80 ±11 %; Low (350–450 °C) 0.65 ±15 %"; applicable only to mineral soils under cropland/grassland. (L2, search extracts; Carbon Management 2024 JIS paper and naro.go.jp reproduce the same values.)
- **Tier-1 kg CO₂ per kg dry char (Fc × Fperm × 3.664):**
  | Feedstock / process | Fc | Fperm | kg CO₂/kg char |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Wood, gasification (>600 °C) | 0.52 | 0.89 | **1.70** |
  | Wood, pyrolysis >600 °C | 0.77 | 0.89 | **2.51** |
  | Wood, pyrolysis 450–600 °C | 0.77 | 0.80 | 2.26 |
  | Rice husk, gasification | 0.13 | 0.89 | **0.42** |
  | Rice husk, pyrolysis >600 °C | 0.49 | 0.89 | 1.60 |
  | Rice husk, pyrolysis 450–600 °C | 0.49 | 0.80 | 1.44 |
  | Herbaceous (corn cob?), gasification | 0.28 | 0.89 | 0.91 |
  | Nut shells, gasification | 0.40 | 0.89 | 1.30 |
  A TLUD/forced-draft gasifier stove is ambiguous between the IPCC "gasification" and "pyrolysis" rows (IPCC's gasification row reflects industrial high-air-ratio gasifiers with very low-C, ash-rich char). Taking both rows: **0.42–2.51 kg CO₂/kg**, versus 3.5 claimed → overstated 1.4×–8×.
- **Certification-style factory-gate values (measured C, L4 search extracts):** EBC/Interholco 86.4 % C → 3.17 t CO₂e/t; CERES 62.8 % C → 2.30; Puro example 2.517. A 95 % C char at 100 % permanence would be 3.48 (= the claim, rounded); with Fperm 0.89 → 3.10. **So "3.5" is simply the A3 figure (95 % C) × 44/12 with no permanence or ash discount — the two claims are one claim.**
- **Measured stove-char carbon (see A3):** TLUD wood char FC 86.2 % (Misginna, Makerere thesis) → gross 3.16, ×0.89 = 2.81; rice-husk TLUD/kiln char 48–51 % FC/TC → gross 1.76–1.87, ×0.89 = 1.56–1.66.
**Loop C (adversarial):**
- Could 3.5 be right on some basis? Yes, only if (a) the char is woody/sawdust-pellet char with ≥95 % C on a **dry** basis (not found anywhere for stove char; best found 86–87 % — see A3), and (b) permanence is ignored (factory-gate EBC style). Under (a)+(b) a 95 % C char gives 3.48. No independent measurement of GaSIO char carbon exists in the public domain to support (a).
- Does any standard credit above 3.5 for a cookstove char? **No.** EBC/CSI count ≤100 % of measured C and then deduct production emissions and (Artisan) 25 % non-aromatic carbon; IPCC applies Fperm; Puro applies H/Corg permanence (Edition 2025: "several centuries", CORC200+); Verra VM0044 v1.2 (27 Jun 2025, ICVCM-approved Aug 2025) applies decay/reversal in §8.4. The Vietnamese press (Báo Nông nghiệp & Môi trường, 29 Aug 2025, L5) notes credit-grade biochar must have **>70 % C, H/C < 0.6, ash < 20 %** — a bar rice-husk char fails on ash alone.
- Could the stove be "pyrolysis" rather than "gasification"? Plausibly yes (TLUD stoves are often called micro-gasifiers but produce char at pyrolysis-like conditions); this lifts the wood-char Tier-1 value from 1.70 to 2.26–2.51, still 28–35 % short of 3.5.
- Queued-but-refused searches that could still move this verdict: Cornelissen 2016 / Pandit 2017 (TLUD vs Kon-Tiki char C %), Njenga 2016 (Kenya gasifier char C %), Nguyen Hong Nam/USTH residual-char papers.
**Best evidence level:** L2 (IPCC defaults) with L1 ceiling
**New verdict:** Overstated — by ~1.1× against the most generous reading (95 % C woody char, no permanence discount), ~1.25× against measured TLUD wood char at factory gate (3.16), 1.4–2.1× against IPCC Tier-1 for wood char, and 2–8× for rice-husk char.
**Confidence:** 0.85 — defaults are L2 and consistent across four independent reproductions; residual uncertainty is only whether GaSIO's own char (unmeasured publicly) approaches 95 % C.
**Change vs prior:** refined — corrected Fc(rice husk, gasification) 0.14 → 0.13; added the pyrolysis-row range (1.17–2.51) and EBC/Puro factory-gate benchmarks (2.3–3.17); showed 3.5 = 0.95 × 44/12 (i.e. A2 is derived from A3).
**What CCS could send to settle it:** the lab certificate behind the 95 % figure (basis: as-received / dry / daf; total C vs fixed C; ash %; H/Corg), the feedstock the char came from, and the permanence/ash assumptions used to get 3.5.
**Sources:** as A1 plus: [Verra VM0044 v1.0 (2022) table reproducing IPCC 4Ap.1](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/VM0044_v1.0_Methodology_for_Biochar.pdf) (search extract) · [Verra VM0044 current (v1.2, 27 Jun 2025) listing](https://verra.org/documents/vm0044-biochar-utilization-in-soil-and-non-soil-applications-clean) (search extract) · [Estimating soil C sequestration with woody and bamboo biochar using JIS M 8812 — Carbon Management 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17583004.2024.2438228) (search extract) · [NARO (Japan) summary of IPCC Fc/Fperm tables](https://www.naro.go.jp/brain/pr_report/reportR6_archive06.pdf) (search extract) · [Puro.earth — biochar permanence (Edition 2025)](https://puro.earth/our-blog/biochar-permanence) (search extract) · [Puro.earth biochar methodology PDF mirror](https://biochar.groups.io/g/main/attachment/32951/2/Puro.earth%20Biochar%20Methodology.pdf) (search extract) · [Báo Nông nghiệp & Môi trường, 29 Aug 2025 — biochar carbon credits](https://van.nongnghiepmoitruong.vn/vietnam-could-earn-billions-of-usd-annually-from-biochar-carbon-credits-d770303.html) (search extract) · [Misginna, Makerere — TLUD-ND charcoal-making stove](https://news.mak.ac.ug/wp-content/uploads/2011/06/Misginna.pdf) (search extract)

### A3 — "Fixed carbon content of the biochar is 95 %" (USTH analysis, 2024)
**Quoted (VN):** "Hàm lượng cacbon cố định trong than sinh học là 95%" — Overview "2. Giới thiệu GaSIO", p.4
**Prior verdict:** Overstated (rice-husk char typically 20–40 % ash, 34–69 % fixed carbon; 95 % only dry-ash-free woody char)
**Loop A (direct):**
- `CCS Vietnam USTH "than sinh học" phân tích hàm lượng cacbon cố định 95%` → **null** (results were unrelated microbiology/CO₂-fixation pages).
- `"than sinh học" "95%" cacbon OR carbon bếp khí hóa` → **null** for carbon content; the only "95 %" hit is a 2015 Da Nang/qucosa paper claiming a *95 % CO₂-emission reduction* for an energy-saving gasifier stove (calculated, not measured) — a possible source of confusion but not a carbon-content figure.
- `"University of Science and Technology of Hanoi" OR USTH biochar gasifier stove carbon analysis CCS GaSIO VCBG` and `"Hanoi University of Science and Technology" GaSIO OR VCBG biochar analysis CCS Oxfam BEST` → **no document links either university to CCS/GaSIO/VCBG.** Found instead: USTH's CleanED lab (Nguyen Hong Nam) publishes on *residual char from commercial gasification* of rice straw (2023) and bagasse (with Le Quy Don TU) — so USTH does have the competence/equipment, making an unpublished 2024 USTH lab test plausible. **Inconsistency found:** CCS's own World Bioenergy Member News credits the biochar analysis to "Hanoi University of Science and Technology" (HUST = Đại học Bách khoa Hà Nội), while the Overview p.4 says USTH (Đại học Khoa học và Công nghệ Hà Nội). Two different universities.
- `bếp khí hóa sinh khối than sinh học thu được hàm lượng cacbon phân tích Đại học Lâm nghiệp OR USTH OR "Bách khoa" 2024 2025` → only the VNUF 2024 design paper (Đỗ Tuấn Anh, vjol 106721; thermal efficiency 21.9 % vs 16.8 %) — no char analysis in the extract.
- Best source: L6 (CCS statement; the USTH/HUST report itself is not public).
**Loop B (benchmark):** Independent proximate/ultimate analyses found (all search extracts):
- **Rice-husk char**
  1. Nsamba et al. 2014, Makerere (TLUD gasifier, rice husks; L3): FC 50.5 ±0.7 → 51.3 ±0.4 %, **ash 33.7 → 36.7 %** as equivalence ratio rises (raw husk FC 5.5 %, ash 9.1 %).
  2. Homchat & Ramphueiphad 2022, *Results in Engineering* (continuous kiln on downdraft gasifier; L3): VM 12.75 %, **FC 48.89 %, ash 36.13 %**, HHV 19.9 MJ/kg, char yield 37 %.
  3. Nguyen Van Hien et al., EGU 2016 abstract / 2018 preprint (Vietnam, "Top-Lid Updraft Drum" TLUD; L3-ish, not peer-reviewed): rice-husk biochar **total C 47.95 %**; wood and bamboo biochar TC > 80 %; RH BET 3.3 m²/g vs 479 m²/g wood.
  4. Other peer-reviewed RH biochar (ScienceDirect/ACS extracts, 2021–2025): ash up to **47.1 %** (silica); FC 20.8 % (one study), 38–69 % across temperature/time, 52–58 % (co-pyrolysis 500–600 °C); ash 16.8–29.4 % at 500–700 °C in another. Vietnamese OU Journal of Science (HCMC Biotech Centre): RH biochar ash *increases* with pyrolysis temperature 350–650 °C.
  → Dry-ash-free FC for RH char: 48.89/(100−36.13) = **76.5 %**; 51/(100−35) = **78.5 %**. Even daf, rice-husk char does not reach 95 %.
- **Woody / sawdust / pellet char**
  1. Misginna, Makerere thesis (TLUD-ND, fuelwood; L4-ish): **FC 86.19 ±2.38 %, VM 11.95 ±2.46 %, ash 1.86 ±0.53 %** → FC daf **87.8 %**.
  2. *Materials* 2020 (PMC7254296; wood and straw pellets, batch pyrolysis; L3): FC 74.0 % (wood char), 76.2 % (straw-pellet char).
  3. *Energies* 2026 (CRD wood waste, 800 °C pilot slow pyrolysis; L3): C 85.11 %, **FC 70.59 %**; post-treatment at 600 °C raises FC to 77–78 %.
  4. Biochar Today (conference paper, Putra et al.; L5): optimised **waste-wood biochar 94.5 % fixed carbon** — the only ≥94 % figure found, for a steelmaking-grade product, basis unstated.
  5. Nguyen Van Hien (above): wood/bamboo TLUD biochar TC > 80 %.
- **Corn-cob char** (L3 extracts): gasified at 800 °C — ash ≈38 %, FC ≈31.8 %; slow pyrolysis 600 °C screw reactor — FC 65.6 %, ash 18.3 %.
- Definition check (Celignis lab page, L5): fixed carbon = residue after heating to 900 °C for 7 min, excluding ash; not interchangeable with elemental C (daf).
**Loop C (adversarial):** On what basis could 95 % be true?
- **Dry-ash-free fixed carbon of a high-temperature woody char:** requires VM_daf ≤ 5 %, i.e. charring well above 700–800 °C with long residence — plausible for a forced-draft pellet stove's char *only* if GaSIO is run on wood/sawdust pellets and the lab reported daf. Best measured TLUD wood char found is 87.8 % daf.
- **"Fixed carbon" mis-translated from "organic carbon as share of total carbon"** (Corg/Ctot ≈ 95 % is routine) — a labelling slip that would make the number true but meaningless for CO₂ accounting.
- **Rice-husk feedstock:** 95 % is unattainable on *any* basis (ash 34–47 % → FC ≤ 51 % dry, ≤ 79 % daf). The GaSIO brochures list rice husk, sawdust, corn cob and pellets as fuels; the figure can therefore describe at most one of the feedstocks.
- Credit-market bar (Báo NN&MT 2025): >70 % C, H/C < 0.6, ash < 20 % — a woody pellet char at 80–86 % C passes; rice-husk char fails on ash.
- Vietnamese `"than sinh học" "hàm lượng cacbon" "90%" OR "95%" than gỗ nhiệt phân nhiệt độ cao` and `than vỏ trấu "hàm lượng tro" "cacbon cố định"` → the latter returned EPU/OU/Hue journal papers (RH pyrolysis, ash rises with temperature) but no FC numbers in extracts; the former was refused (budget).
**Best evidence level:** L3 (multiple peer-reviewed proximate analyses)
**New verdict:** Overstated — 95 % is unattainable for rice-husk char on any basis (≤51 % dry / ≤79 % daf) and exceeds every measured TLUD/pellet wood char found (74–86 % dry, ~88 % daf); it is reachable only as a daf value for a high-temperature woody char or as Corg/Ctot. Also flag: CCS names two different universities (USTH vs HUST) for the same analysis.
**Confidence:** 0.80 — benchmark evidence is L3 and consistent; residual chance that a daf measurement of GaSIO pellet char really reads ~95 %.
**Change vs prior:** refined — same verdict; added three RH and five woody datapoints with daf conversions, the USTH/HUST naming inconsistency, and the finding that USTH (CleanED) does publish on gasification char so a lab test is plausible but unpublished.
**What CCS could send to settle it:** the 2024 USTH (or HUST) certificate: method (ASTM D1762 / ISO 17246 / ultimate analysis), basis (ar/dry/daf), ash %, VM %, Corg, H/Corg, feedstock tested, stove model and air setting.

- **Ratios:** 8,000 ÷ 1,100 (raw husk) = **7.3×**; ÷ 2,150 (husk pellet) = 3.7×; ÷ 1,650 (củi trấu) = 4.8×; ÷ 2,300 (sawdust briquette) = 3.5×. So "4–5×" matches a *per-kg* comparison against **densified** fuel (pellets/briquettes), not raw husk.
- **Yield-adjusted:** stove char yield is 16.6–18 % of fuel mass (Gitau et al. 2019, *Energies* 12:4285, L3; ICRAF Embu project 17–18 %) → biochar revenue per kg of *fuel* = 8,000 × 0.17–0.25 = **1,360–2,000 VND**, i.e. roughly 0.6–1.8× the fuel's own price, not 4–5×.
**Loop C (adversarial):**
- Could "international" be defensible? If CCS meant *credit-backed* export-grade biochar, international realised values are higher, not lower, than 8 M VND/t (IBI USD 543/t avg; Europe up to €2,000/t) — so the number *understates* international prices while *overstating* that it is "high". If CCS meant Vietnamese *export* FOB, no data found (customs HS code search queued for loop 2).
- Could 8 M VND/t be a 2024 BiocharVN or Huucovang list price (prior hypothesis)? Not confirmable this loop (both sites blocked; BiocharVN's indexed article gives only a range). The Khánh Hòa 2022 range 6,000–8,500 VND/kg independently brackets it.
- Could "4–5×" be true? Yes on a per-kg basis versus pelletised/briquetted fuel (3.5–4.8×). Misleading because it ignores the ~17–25 % mass yield and because GaSIO users feed raw husk/sawdust too (7× / unknown).
- Southeast-Asia comparators (Thailand/Indonesia/Philippines/Cambodia USD/t, 2024–25): **null** — only a 2001 Thai husk price (402–450 THB/t) and Singapore husk export data surfaced.
- Nitidae price report: **null** in two searches (one refused).
**Best evidence level:** L5 (IBI 2025 report, Vietnamese press, CTCN project file) — no L4 statistics on biochar prices exist.
**New verdict:** Contradicted (label) — 8 M VND/t (~USD 300–330/t) is a mid-range **domestic Vietnamese** price (independent VN sources 4,000–20,000 VND/kg), not an international premium (international product prices USD 300–2,000/t, IBI 2025 implied avg USD 543/t). The "4–5× input" ratio is Plausible only per kg versus pelletised fuel (3.5–4.8×) and collapses to ~0.6–1.8× once char yield is included.
**Confidence:** 0.75 — many L5 datapoints agree on the domestic range; the IBI figure is the only international benchmark with a 2025 date and it blends revenue sources; the exact CCS source of "8 triệu" is still unknown.
**Change vs prior:** refined — prior "wrong label" is upheld and quantified; BiocharVN attribution could not be re-confirmed (site blocked); added CTCN 2015–16 4,000 VND/kg with "no demand", FAO 500–12,000 range, IBI 2025 USD 543/t, feedstock prices and the yield-adjusted ratio.

## 1. Method and limitations

Three passes were run on 34 claims: loop 1 verified each claim with three searches (direct / benchmark / adversarial); loop 2 red-teamed every loop-1 verdict, trying to overturn it; loop 3 closed remaining gaps and checked every quote and page against the PDFs. Evidence is graded L1 (arithmetic/physics) to L6 (company marketing); verdicts use the PROTOCOL scale (Confirmed / Plausible / Overstated / Contradicted / Impossible / Unverifiable) with a 0–1 confidence.

The sandbox blocked almost every web host (DNS failure or proxy 403), so most citations are **search extracts** — a search engine's snippet of a page, not the page itself. They must be re-opened before any figure is quoted externally. Primary sources that **were opened**: the eight CCS PDFs (Google Drive connector); the IPCC 2019 Refinement Vol. 4 Ch. 2 Appendix 4 text (GitHub mirror); Aprovecho's ISO 19867-1 processing code and ISO/TR 19867-3 tier dump (GitHub); a Clean Cooking Catalog scrape (474 stoves); OWID energy/CO₂ datasets carrying EI 2025 and Global Carbon Budget data; two Verra registry dumps; and the Oxfam/CCS 2021 VCBG brochure (S3). GitHub links below point to the repositories named in the loop notes (files were read via raw.githubusercontent.com). Everything else is marked "(search extract)".

## 3. Where our earlier documents were wrong or too harsh

Every point where the PROTOCOL verdict (or our earlier write-ups) was overturned, softened or re-attributed. Use these to correct the CCS feedback document and the funder report.

1. **B2 — 83.7 % can be a legitimate ISO 19867-1 number.** Loop 1 wrote that 83.7 % "cannot be a legitimate ISO 19867-1 figure". Loop 2 overturned this: ISO 19867-1 §5.4.5 defines a "thermal efficiency with energy credit for remaining char", and Aprovecho's reference implementation tiers that value too. A 50 % no-credit stove leaving 40 % of fuel energy as char (≈23 % char yield — normal for a TLUD) reports exactly 83.7 %. The correct criticism is "basis not stated / headline misleading", not "impossible". The "Contradicted by CCS's own 67 % and 50 %" line must be dropped: the three numbers may be three bases (hot-start WBT, ISO no-credit, ISO with-credit).
2. **B1 — the Mimi Moto comparison was made on an unknown basis.** We compared GaSIO's "Tier 5 hiệu suất" with Mimi Moto's "Tier 5 / 50 %" (CCA investor page); the Clean Cooking Catalog lists Mimi Moto at 49.7 % = Tier 4. Neither value's char basis is known, so "matches Mimi Moto" / "equals the best stove" should be replaced by "needs the efficiency basis to compare".
3. **F2 — "160 triệu tấn" is confirmed.** PROTOCOL said "needs a source"; MARD (TS Tống Xuân Chinh, 10-09-2021, GSO 2020 data) gives 156.8 Mt total by-products (crop 88.9 + livestock 61.4 + forestry 5.5 + fisheries ≈1). Our earlier comparison of 160 Mt with crop residues (79–98 Mt) compared the wrong aggregates — the crop-residue figure should be compared with the 65 Mt sub-figure. The 24 Mtoe is arithmetically consistent with 65 Mt (×15.5 GJ/t).
4. **C1 — the 1,027 kg/yr figure is the conservative one, and "1.027×10 ≠ 8" was unfair.** 1,027 kg/yr reproduces a TOOL30-default calculation at fNRB 0.30–0.36 and sits inside the only measured Vietnamese household baseline (Pu Hu, 1.2–2.3 t wood/hh/yr). 8 t over a 10-year life is 1,027 kg × 7.8 effective stove-years — a standard usage drop-off, not an arithmetic error. The criticism belongs to the Household flyer's 3–5 t/yr only. Also: the p.2 sentence sits under "Tích lũy Cacbon trong khí quyển vào trong đất", so 1,027 kg may be the biochar component alone.

6. **C5 — bánh chưng 2.2 t CO₂/t is plausible under a boiler baseline.** Loop 1's "Overstated 4–7×" used household-pot fuel data; commercial villages (Tranh Khúc, Hùng Lô) boil ≈1,000 cakes per batch for 8–10 h in steam boilers, where a coal-boiler model gives 1.0–2.1 t/t.

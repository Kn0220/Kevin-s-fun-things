## Group B

### B2 — "Hiệu suất tối đa của bếp dân sinh là 83,7%"
**Loop-1 verdict:** Overstated as a cooking/ISO thermal efficiency and Contradicted by CCS's own 67 % (Delft 2015 / BEST 2024) and 50 % (LinkedIn); loop 1 held that 83.7 % "cannot be a legitimate ISO 19867-1 figure" (confidence 0.80).
**Red-team case:** ISO 19867-1 defines **two** thermal efficiencies — Clause 5.4.4 "with no energy credit for remaining char" and Clause 5.4.5 "with energy credit for remaining char". For a char-making stove the second is much larger than the first. If the CREEC test reported both, "83,7 %" may be the §5.4.5 figure of the very same test whose §5.4.4 figure is the "50 %" on LinkedIn — making 83.7 % a legitimate ISO 19867-1 number, though a misleading one to headline.
**Tests:**
1. *Which basis does ISO/TR 19867-3 tier?* `WebSearch` ×4 on the TR/char-credit question → the TR text is paywalled; ANSI previews (search extract, L2) confirm only the clause structure: 5.4.4 "Cooking thermal efficiency with no energy credit for remaining char", 5.4.5 "…with energy credit for remaining char", 5.4.6 "Char energy productivity", 5.4.7 "Char mass productivity". **Primary (opened):** Aprovecho's ISO 19867-1 processing script `LEMS/LEMS_EnergyCalcs_ISO.py` (commit 717c47e) computes `eff_wo_char` ("Clause 5.4.4 Formula 6: eff=Q1/B/Qnet,af*100"), `eff_w_char` ("Clause 5.4.5 Formula 7: eff=Q1/(B*Qnet,af-C*Qnet,char)*100"), `char_energy_productivity` ("Clause 5.4.6 Formula 8: Echar=C*Qnet,char/B/Qnet,af*100") and `char_mass_productivity` ("Clause 5.4.7 Formula 9: mchar=C/B*100"), then assigns **a tier to each efficiency separately** (`tier_eff_wo_char` and `tier_eff_w_char`, both: <10 Tier 0 … ≥50 Tier 5). The same repo's `iso_tiers_dump.txt` reproduces the ISO/TR 19867-3 table (Tier 5: ≥50 %, CO ≤3.0 g/MJd, PM2.5 ≤5 mg/MJd, safety ≥95, durability <10; Tier 4: ≥40, ≤4.4, ≤62, ≥86, <15; Tier 3: ≥30, ≤7.2, ≤218, ≥77, <20; Tier 2: ≥20, ≤11.5, ≤481, ≥68, <25; Tier 1: ≥10, ≤18.3, ≤1030, ≥60, <35; Tier 0 below). **So an accredited lab's software reports a "Tier" for the char-credited efficiency as well; the TR does not, on the evidence available, force one basis.** (L4: a testing lab's published implementation of the standard; the TR text itself remains unseen.) The code's "pending ISO updates" block computes a test-total efficiency only as `eff_w_char_total`, hinting that the revision in progress (ISO/DIS 19867-1) leans toward the char-credited total — recorded as an observation, not a finding.
2. *Arithmetic (L1), using Formula 7.* η_w = η_wo / (1 − E_char). If 50 % (LinkedIn) and 83.7 % (Overview) are the two ISO efficiencies of one test: 1 − 50/83.7 = **0.403 → char energy productivity 40 %**, i.e. char mass productivity ≈ 0.403 × 17/30 = **23 %** (pellets 17 MJ/kg, char LHV ~30 MJ/kg) or ≈ 29 % for rice husk (13 MJ/kg fuel, ~18 MJ/kg ash-rich char). Measured TLUD char yields are 17–18 % (Gitau et al. 2025, Sustainability 17:5872, search extract, L3), 19–23 % (Nielsen, Bio-Char II, L5), 22 % dry (Cornelissen et al. 2016, PLOS ONE, L3), 37 % for rice husk (Homchat 2022, L3). **The pair (50 %, 83.7 %) is internally consistent with a normal char-making stove.**
3. *Any published ISO 19867-1 result with char-credited efficiency >70 %?* `WebSearch` ×3 → **null.** Found instead: Tanzanian natural-draft TLUD "charcoal production efficiency" 36–48 % with 18.5 % TE (UDSM, L4); FD TLUD pellet 26.7 % TE / 58.6 % cold-gas (Springer 2023, L3); Champion et al. 2025 (ES&T, ISO 19867-1 on three pellet semi-gasifiers × four pellets: PM2.5 Tier 1–4, CO Tier 2–5; efficiency values not in extract, L3); Kenya "65–73 %" preprint rs-198040 **not retrievable** (two searches, null). The only >80 % ISO-metric value seen anywhere is Aprovecho's own test file for an **alcohol** stove (86.9 %, `alcohol data/L2_dict.txt`, opened) — irrelevant to biomass but proof that the metric itself can exceed 80 %.
4. *CCS's own figures.* ccspin.org (blocked) — but Genk (2 Jun 2017, search extract, L6 press of CCS statements) reports the **TU Delft** result as "57 % khi bắt đầu thử lúc lò nguội, 67 % khi lò đã nóng sẵn, và khoảng 48 % khi đun ở nhiệt độ vừa" — so the 67 % is a hot-start, high-power value, basis (char credit) unstated; the 27 Nov 2015 CCS workshop at Trung tâm Việt Đức, ĐH Bách khoa was about "bếp khí hóa sinh khối hiệu suất cao dùng nhiên liệu viên nén" (tietkiemnangluong.com.vn, L5). The BEST "98 % / 67 % / 85 %" sentence and the LinkedIn "chứng nhận hiệu suất 50 %" could **not** be re-opened or re-found this loop (switch-asia, ngocentre, linkedin blocked; two targeted searches null) — they stand as loop-1 extracts. `"83,7%" bếp dân sinh GaSIO` → null (the VnExpress contest entry from the prior pass was not re-surfaced).
**Outcome: SHARPENED (loop-1 sub-claim overturned).** New verdict: **Plausible as the ISO 19867-1 §5.4.5 "thermal efficiency with energy credit for remaining char"; Overstated as presented** ("hiệu suất tối đa" with no basis stated, while the comparable no-credit figure would be ≈50 %, and the 98 %-to-syngas statement in BEST documents is incompatible with the ~23 % char yield this reading requires). The "Contradicted by 67 %/50 %" element is downgraded to **Unresolved** — three numbers on possibly three bases (hot-start WBT; ISO no-credit; ISO with-credit) are not necessarily contradictory. Confidence **0.70** (the metric exists and the arithmetic fits; no published >70 % example and no CREEC page seen).
**Settling document CCS could provide:** the CREEC report page with §5.4.4 and §5.4.5 efficiencies, char mass/energy productivity (Formulas 8–9), fuel, phases and 90 % confidence intervals; plus which basis the "Tier 5 hiệu suất" on p.4 refers to.

The field observation window was therefore ≤ ~18 months, yet the reported lives are 25.2–55.7 months. **The figures must be projections (e.g. wear-rate extrapolation), not observed lifetimes.**

The "stove minimum" is the weakest listed component (mặt sơ cấp, 31.5 months) with a 20 % safety factor — a standard engineering projection convention. It tells us the 25.2 is derived, and that the method is component-wear projection; it does not tell us the time basis.
- **Duty-cycle test of the loop-1 hypothesis:** 25.2 months × 730.5 h = **18,400 h** if continuous-equivalent. Tea-sector duty evidence found: a Thái Nguyên cooperative reports **7–9 harvest batches per year**; households finish the last batch in late October; a factory runs 2–3 shifts May–October (search extracts, L5). No household roasting-hours figure was found (1 search, null). Household roasting of ~3–6 h/day on ~150–250 days/yr gives ~500–1,500 h/yr → 18,400 h = **12–37 years**; even at 2,000–3,000 h/yr it is 6–9 years. So **if** "tháng vận hành" means continuous-equivalent operation, "10 năm" is comfortably consistent; **if** it means calendar months in service at actual duty, 25.2 months = 2.1 years and the two statements conflict. Both readings survive; the engineering convention (wear per operating hour, expressed in months) favours the first, the plain Vietnamese reading favours the second.
**Outcome: SHARPENED — Contradicted as worded, reconcilable; confidence 0.55 → 0.60** (new: study dated Oct 2023 → projections; 25.2 = 0.8 × 31.5 reveals the method; duty arithmetic bounds the continuous reading at 6–37 years).

### A1 — "1 kg than sinh học giúp chôn xuống đất 5,6kg cacbonic"
**Loop-1 verdict:** Impossible as a storage statement (0.97); only rescuable as a mislabelled lifecycle figure (1.4 kg CO₂e/kg biomass ÷ 25 % yield = 5.6).
**Red-team case:** (a) the "5,6" might be an extraction error for "2,6" or "3,6"; (b) CCS may be quoting a lifecycle "avoided + stored" factor that is legitimate on its own terms.
**Tests:**
- **Primary PDF (Drive connector):** the Service-stove flyer reads exactly "1 kg than sinh học giúp chôn xuống đất 5,6kg cacbonic". **Not an extraction error.** The same flyer's other impact numbers are the Household flyer's scaled by **1.5×** (3–5 → 4.5–7.5 t CO₂/stove/yr; 30–50 → 45–75 t lifetime), and the per-kg figure is scaled by **1.6×** (3.5 → 5.6). A per-kilogram-of-biochar factor cannot depend on stove size. **New hypothesis (c): 5.6 is an editorial artefact of scaling the household flyer's figures for the larger stove**, not a calculation. This is the most economical origin found.
- **A1-origin test (loop-1 hypothesis):** `Kane et al. 2024 ERL` → confirmed (search extract, L3): "Uncertainty in determining carbon dioxide removal potential of biochar", *Environ. Res. Lett.* 20(1), DOI 10.1088/1748-9326/ad99e9 — best-practice cradle-to-gate removal **median 1.4 kg CO₂e per kg biomass**; poor practice median **+0.090 kg CO₂e/kg** (net emission, 66 % probability); prior literature range 0.20–1.3. This is for industrial pyrolysis with bio-oil co-products, not stoves. 1.4 ÷ 0.25 = 5.6 remains an **arithmetical coincidence with no trace in any CCS or Oxfam/BEST text** (searches for a CCS "1,4 kg" or "25 %" statement: null). Stove-specific LCAs found (Sundberg et al. 2020, *Mitig. Adapt. Strateg. Glob. Change*; Smebye et al. 2017, *Biomass & Bioenergy* 101:35–43, functional unit 1 kg biochar; Whitman 2011) do not report a 5.6 figure in any extract.
- **Stoichiometry (L1) re-stated:** 5.6 ÷ 3.664 = **1.53 kg C per kg char (153 %)**.
**Outcome: UPHELD — Impossible (as storage), confidence 0.97.** Sharpened: wording confirmed from the PDF; new, better-fitting origin (1.5–1.6× scaling of the household flyer); the 1.4 kg CO₂e/kg biomass figure is now anchored to Kane et al. 2024 but remains unconnected to CCS.
**Settling document:** CCS's spreadsheet behind the Service flyer, showing whether the biochar line was computed or scaled.

### A2 — "1kg than sinh học giúp chôn xuống đất 3,5kg cacbonic"
**Loop-1 verdict:** Overstated 1.1× (95 % C woody char, no permanence) to 8× (rice-husk gasification char, IPCC Tier 1); 3.5 = 0.95 × 44/12 (0.85).
**Red-team case:** loop 1 relied on second-hand reproductions of the IPCC tables; if the real table differs (e.g. higher Fc for gasification char) or if Verra/Puro credit without a permanence discount, 3.5 could be within range.
**Tests:**
- **IPCC 2019 Refinement, Vol. 4, Ch. 2, Appendix 4 — opened (text mirror of the official PDF, L2 primary).** Definition: "biochar is defined as a solid material generated by heating biomass to a temperature in excess of 350°C under conditions of controlled and limited oxidant concentrations to prevent combustion. These processes can be classified as either pyrolysis (in which oxidants are excluded), or gasification (in which oxidant concentrations are low enough to generate syngas)." Scope: "only applicable for biochar added to mineral soils in grasslands and croplands … not applicable for application of biochar to soils in forest land, settlements, other lands or wetlands." **Equation 4Ap.1** (p. Ap4.1–2): ΔBC_mineral = Σ_p (BC_TOT,p × FC_p × Fperm_p) × 44/12 with BC_TOT = "mass of biochar incorporated into mineral soil during the inventory year for each biochar production type p, tonnes biochar dry matter yr⁻¹", FC_p = "organic carbon content of biochar … tonnes C tonne⁻¹ biochar dry matter, Table 4Ap.1", Fperm_p = "fraction of biochar carbon … remaining (unmineralised) after 100 years, Table 4Ap.2". **Table 4Ap.1 (verbatim values, mean ± 95 % CL as % of mean):** Animal manure — Pyrolysis 0.38 ± 49 %, Gasification 0.09 ± 53 %; **Wood — Pyrolysis 0.77 ± 42 %, Gasification 0.52 ± 52 %**; Herbaceous (grasses, forbs, leaves; excluding rice husks and rice straw) — Pyrolysis 0.65 ± 45 %, Gasification 0.28 ± 50 %; **Rice husks and rice straw — Pyrolysis 0.49 ± 41 %, Gasification 0.13 ± 50 %**; Nut shells, pits and stones — Pyrolysis 0.74 ± 39 %, Gasification 0.40 ± 52 %; Biosolids — Pyrolysis 0.35 ± 40 %, Gasification 0.07 ± 50 %. Source note: "FC_p was calculated using the organic carbon content of biochar on a dry ash-free (daf) basis according to equation 14 from Neves et al. (2011) … corrected for ash content … using the regression equation … of biochar yield from Woolf et al. (2014)." **Table 4Ap.2:** "High temperature pyrolysis and gasification (> 600 °C) 0.89 ± 13 %; Medium temperature pyrolysis (450-600 °C) 0.80 ± 11 %; Low (350-450 °C) 0.65 ± 15 %" (bootstrap CL). Loop 1's values are **confirmed exactly** (incl. the rice-husk gasification correction 0.14 → 0.13; the ± for rice-husk pyrolysis is 41 %, which loop 1 omitted).
- **Tier-1 kg CO₂ per kg dry char for every GaSIO-listed feedstock (L1 on L2):** wood/cinnamon branches/sawdust pellets — pyrolysis >600 °C **2.51**, 450–600 °C 2.26, gasification **1.70**; rice husk — pyrolysis >600 °C 1.60, gasification **0.42**; corn cob (IPCC "herbaceous") — pyrolysis >600 °C 2.12, gasification 0.91; peanut shell (IPCC "nut shells") — pyrolysis >600 °C 2.41, gasification 1.30. Claim 3.5 exceeds every cell by 1.4–8.3×. 3.5 ÷ 3.664 = **0.955 → requires 95.5 % C and Fperm = 1.00**.
- **Verra VM0044:** `"VM0044" "v1.2"` → confirmed: v1.2 **active 27 June 2025** (verra.org methodology page), ICVCM CCP approval announced **13 August 2025** (search extracts, L5); v1.0 Oct 2022, v1.1 Jul 2023, v2.0 in preparation. The permanence clause text was not retrievable (verra.org blocked). **Puro.earth:** Edition 2025 raises durability to "several centuries (CORC200+)"; H/Corg limit 0.7 in the 2023 version; permanence equations follow Woolf et al. 2021 with H/Corg and soil temperature (puro.earth / biochartoday extracts, L5). Neither credits 100 % of measured carbon with no permanence or production-emission deduction; neither can get a 95 % C char to 3.5.
**Outcome: UPHELD — Overstated (1.1× to 8× depending on feedstock/process), confidence 0.85 → 0.90** (IPCC tables now read in the primary text).
**Settling document:** as loop 1 (USTH certificate basis and the permanence assumption behind 3.5).

### A3 — "Hàm lượng cacbon cố định trong than sinh học là 95%" (USTH, 2024)
**Loop-1 verdict:** Overstated; 95 % reachable only as daf high-T woody char or as Corg/Ctot; flagged USTH vs HUST naming (0.80).
**Red-team case:** (a) the "two universities" flag may be spurious; (b) with pellets as the stated fuel, a 95 % fixed-carbon char on a dry basis might be attainable and reported by a competent lab.
**Tests:**
- **USTH vs HUST — resolved as a translation artefact (hypothesis c, strong).** Primary PDF: both p.4 and p.18 say "trường Đại học Khoa học và Công nghệ Hà Nội" = **USTH** (University of Science and Technology of Hanoi). HUST's Vietnamese name, "Đại học Bách khoa Hà Nội", appears nowhere in CCS material. CCS's English texts (WBA May 2025; CTC-N profile — both only as loop-1 extracts, pages blocked) say "Hanoi University of Science and Technology", which is HUST's official English name **but also the word-for-word translation of USTH's Vietnamese name**. One university (USTH), mistranslated in English. USTH's CleanED group (Nguyen Hong Nam) publishes on "residual char after gasification" of rice straw (2023, high ash, 438 m²/g) and bagasse (low ash, 749 m²/g) (vjol.info.vn, search extracts, L3) — competence confirmed; **neither paper mentions CCS/GaSIO** and neither extract gives fixed-carbon values.
- **Peer-reviewed proximate analyses of gasifier/TLUD char, dry basis (Loop B, ≥3 required):**
  1. Curay et al. 2020, *Annals of Tropical Research* 42(2), DOI 10.32945/atr42210.2020 (UP Diliman; coconut-shell charcoal, five methods): **TLUD-Top FC 64.77 %** (VM 20.65 %), **TLUD-Bottom FC 45.85 %**, drum kiln 54.63 %, pit 56.84 %, Hookway 56.59 % (L3).
  2. Cornelissen et al. 2016, *PLOS ONE* 11:e0154617 (Nepal, flame-curtain and other kilns incl. TLUD): biochar **C 76 ± 9 % (n = 57)**, char yield 22 % dry / 40 % carbon basis, EBC-compliant (L3; total C, not FC).
  3. *Materials* 2020 (PMC7254296): wood-pellet char **FC 74.0 %**, straw-pellet 76.2 % (L3, loop 1).
  4. *Energies* 2026 (DOI 10.3390/en19133097): CRD wood, 800 °C slow pyrolysis **FC 70.6 %**, 77–78 % after 600 °C post-treatment (L3, loop 1).
  5. Misginna & Rajabu (UDSM/Makerere): eucalyptus TLUD-ND **FC 86.19 ± 2.38 %**, VM 11.95 %, ash 1.86 %, HHV 32.33 MJ/kg (thesis + conference paper, L4) — the highest stove-char FC found.
  6. Nsamba et al. 2014 (Makerere, rice husk TLUD): FC 50.5–51.3 %, ash 33.7–36.7 % (L3); Homchat & Ramphueiphad 2022: RH FC 48.89 %, ash 36.13 % (L3).
  7. Masís-Meléndez et al. 2020, *Applied Sciences* 10:507 (TLUD pinewood and Guadua bamboo; proximate analysis performed, values not in extract — pinewood chars "showed similar properties", bamboo more variable) (L3, values pending). Gitau et al. 2025, *Sustainability* 17:5872: Embu char had "the highest calorific value and fixed carbon, lowest ash" — values not in extract (L3, pending).
  **No wood, sawdust, pellet, coconut, corn-cob or rice-husk stove char in the literature reaches 95 % fixed carbon on a dry basis; the ceiling found is ~86 % (eucalyptus TLUD-ND), typical 46–77 %.** Rice husk is capped near 50 % by silica ash. Peanut shell and corn cob TLUD data: not found (null, two searches).
**Outcome: UPHELD — Overstated, confidence 0.80 → 0.85.** Sharpened: the "two universities" flag is withdrawn and replaced by "English mistranslation of USTH"; three more L3 datapoints (64.8 %, 45.9 %, 76 % C) added; 95 % remains reachable only as daf-FC of a high-temperature woody char or as Corg/Ctot.
**Settling document:** the 2024 USTH certificate (method, basis ar/dry/daf, ash, VM, Corg, H/Corg, feedstock, stove setting).

## Q0. Sanity check of the company's headline claims

### Takeaway
Two of the six claims hold up: ~75% fossil fuel share and the 3.5 kg CO2 per kg biochar *upper bound*. Two are mis-labelled: the 8M VND/t biochar price is a **Vietnamese domestic** list price, not an international one, and the 60,000 deaths figure covers **all air pollution**, not household air pollution. The 160 Mt / 65 Mt residue figures could not be traced to any source; the best-documented figures are 79 to 98 Mt of crop residues. 3.5 kg CO2/kg is a theoretical gross maximum. Real certified net removals per kg are much lower, especially for high-ash rice-husk char.

### Cited Findings
- **Claim "160 Mt/yr residues, ~65 Mt burned/discarded, 24 Mtoe (~USD 28 bn)":** No source found for 160 Mt. Documented totals are lower:
  - MOIT (2017): 79 Mt of agricultural residues (rice husk and straw, corn, cassava, coconut, sugarcane) — [FFTC-AP](https://ap.fftc.org.tw/article/2786)
  - Institute of Agricultural Environment survey (2019): crop residues and by-products of 95–98 Mt/yr — [FFTC-AP](https://ap.fftc.org.tw/article/2786)
  - Institute of Energy (undated, older): 53 Mt of agricultural waste ≈ 12 Mtoe — [b-company.jp](https://b-company.jp/current-status-and-potential-of-biomass-energy-in-vietnam/)
- One academic study (Beňová et al., 2021, *Agronomy* 11(1):169) uses high residue ratios. It puts rice straw at 97 Mt/yr (>380 TWh) and rice husk at 9 Mt/yr (35 TWh). If forestry residues are added, this could get close to 160 Mt — [MDPI Agronomy](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4395/11/1/169); other studies put rice straw at ~45–54 Mt — [Nature Sci. Rep.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-80678-3)
- **Claim "~75% of fuel consumed is fossil":** The Energy Institute Statistical Review 2024 (2023 data) puts Vietnam's primary energy at 4.89 EJ: coal 47.4%, oil 24.5%, gas 5.3%, hydro 15.4%, other renewables 7.4%. That makes fossil ≈ 77% of primary energy — [aenert.com summarising the Statistical Review](https://aenert.com/countries/asia/energy-industry-in-vietnam/). The IEA puts oil products at 33% of total final consumption and electricity at 31% (2023), with industry taking 50% of final consumption — [IEA Viet Nam](https://www.iea.org/countries/viet-nam/energy-mix)
- **Claim "biochar sells ~8M VND/tonne internationally":** This is a domestic Vietnamese price. Examples:
  - BiocharVN lists rice-husk biochar at 8,000,000 VND/t, and Hữu Cơ Vàng lists bamboo biochar at 10,000,000 VND/t — [BiocharVN](https://biocharvn.com/en); [Hữu Cơ Vàng](https://huucovang.com/gia-biochar-tren-thi-truong/)
  - Retail is 6,000–12,000 VND/kg, and up to 15,000–20,000 VND/kg when blended with microbes or compost — [Hữu Cơ Vàng](https://huucovang.com/gia-biochar-tren-thi-truong/)
  - High-quality cacao-husk biochar in Đắk Lắk sells for ~15,000 VND/kg domestically — [VnExpress](https://vnexpress.net/nong-dan-dak-lak-hoc-lam-than-sinh-hoc-tu-vo-cacao-4862400.html)
  - A planned Mekong rice-husk biochar plant expects USD 300–400/t from product sales and ~USD 500/t including carbon credits — [VnExpress](https://vnexpress.net/viet-nam-sap-co-nha-may-than-sinh-hoc-dau-tien-tu-vo-trau-4872362.html)
- **Claim "1 kg biochar sequesters 3.5 kg CO2":** The standard conversion is carbon mass × 44/12 (3.664–3.667), multiplied by a permanence/stability factor, minus process, transport and application emissions — [EDF protocol comparison](https://www.edf.org/comparison-biochar-carbon-market-protocols); [Senken buyer's guide](https://www.senken.io/academy/biochar)
  - Puro bases its permanence factor on H:Corg, soil temperature and a 200- or 1,000-year horizon — [EDF](https://www.edf.org/comparison-biochar-carbon-market-protocols)
  - Isometric credits only the highly durable fraction — [Isometric Biochar v1.3](https://registry.isometric.com/protocol/biochar/1.3)
  - Puro and EBC both require H/Corg < 0.7 — [Stasis Carbon blog](https://www.stasiscarbon.com/blog/biochar-permanence.html)
- **Claim "household air pollution causes ~60,000 deaths/yr in Vietnam":** WHO has been reported as attributing 60,000 deaths/yr in Vietnam to air pollution overall — [NGO Centre citing WHO](https://ngocentre.org.vn/news/vietnam-records-60000-deaths-linked-air-pollutionyear-who). WHO Vietnam's current page cites State of Global Air 2024 for "at least 70,000" air-pollution deaths/yr, ambient and household combined, and names household solid fuel as a major contributor — [WHO Vietnam](https://www.who.int/vietnam/health-topics/air-pollution). The only Vietnam household-only figure found is old: 23,800 deaths/yr in a WHO 2004 country estimate — [J. Health Res. (Thai journal)](https://he01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/jhealthres/article/download/92845/72687/230749)

### Inferences
- **Residues:** 160 Mt is plausible only as a theoretical potential that includes forestry residues, high straw-to-grain ratios and possibly manure. A defensible pitch figure is "~80–100 Mt/yr of crop residues (MOIT 2017; IAE 2019)". The 65 Mt "burned or discarded" share is directionally consistent with the evidence:
  - Rice straw alone is ~45–54 Mt.
  - Roughly 45–85% of Mekong farmers burn straw depending on season (see Q3).
- **Energy value:** 24 Mtoe ≈ USD 28 bn implies ~USD 1,170/toe. That is roughly the value of retail LPG or diesel, not crude oil or coal. It is an optimistic replacement-value framing and should be labelled that way.
- **Biochar price:** The "4–5x the biomass input price" claim cannot be checked; no rice-husk price was found (gap). Typical pyrolysis/gasification char yields are ~20–35% by mass (general literature; not verified in this search). A 4–5x per-tonne price premium therefore means only ~1–1.75x revenue per tonne of feedstock, before carbon credits.
- **CO2 per kg:** At 95% C, 0.95 × 3.667 = 3.48 kg CO2, so 3.5 is the theoretical gross ceiling.
  - Typical carbon content is roughly 40–55% for rice-husk char (high silica ash) and 70–85% for wood char. These ranges are general knowledge, not verified in this search.
  - Net certified removals would then be roughly 1.2–1.8 tCO2e per t of husk char and ~2.0–2.8 tCO2e per t of wood char, after permanence factors (~0.75–0.9) and project emissions.
  - The pitch should say "up to ~3.5 kg CO2 (theoretical); ~1.5–2.8 kg certified net, depending on feedstock".
- **Deaths:** The pitch should say "air pollution causes 60,000–70,000 deaths/yr in Vietnam (WHO/SoGA 2024), with household solid-fuel smoke a major contributor". It should not attribute all of them to household air pollution.

### Gaps
- Original source of the 160 Mt / 65 Mt / 24 Mtoe / USD 28 bn figures. It may be a Vietnamese conference paper or MOIT/IE presentation (2022) not indexed in English search.
- A current, household-only HAP death count for Vietnam. The State of Global Air country table and IHME GBD were not reachable.
- Domestic rice-husk, sawdust and coffee-husk prices (VND/t) needed to test the 4–5x claim.

## Q1. Clean cooking: households using solid biomass, market size, finance flows

### Takeaway
About 2.1 bn people globally (2023) and ~120 m in Southeast Asia (IEA, 2026) still lack clean cooking. In Vietnam, roughly 22% of people still use traditional biomass stoves (Tier 0–1) and ~25–30% use polluting fuels, though the data is dated. Clean-cooking enterprise finance is small: ~USD 0.2 bn/yr tracked against an USD 8 bn/yr need. The IEA Paris summit pledges (USD 2.2 bn) are almost entirely Africa-focused, which is a headwind for an Asian entrant seeking concessional capital.

### Cited Findings
- **Global access (Tracking SDG7 2025, 2023 data):**
  - ~2.1 bn people (range 1.9–2.5 bn) relied on polluting cooking fuels; 74% (70–77%) used clean fuels.
  - Population growth outpaced gains, and there was little to no change between 2022 and 2023.
  - On current trends, 78% access by 2030, leaving ~1.8 bn without.
  - The top 20 deficit countries account for >70% of people without access.
  — [IRENA/Tracking SDG7 2025](https://www.irena.org/Publications/2025/Jun/Tracking-SDG-7-The-Energy-Progress-Report-2025); [UN SDG7 progress](https://www.un.org/en/node/208821)
- State of Global Air's HAP page uses a broader solid-fuel measure: in 2023, 33% of the global population (~2.6 bn) relied on solid fuels for cooking, and HAP caused 2.8 m deaths — [State of Global Air HAP](https://www.stateofglobalair.org/pollution-sources/hap)
- **Southeast Asia:**
  - IEA Southeast Asia Energy Outlook 2026: clean cooking still lags, with **120 million people (almost one-fifth of the region)** lacking access — [IEA SEAO 2026 exec summary](https://www.iea.org/reports/southeast-asia-energy-outlook-2026/executive-summary)
  - SEAO 2024: ~20% of the population, >100 m people on traditional biomass, coal or kerosene. The largest gaps are in Lao PDR, Myanmar and Cambodia, and the energy-price crisis was a setback — [IEA SEAO 2024](https://www.iea.org/reports/southeast-asia-energy-outlook-2024/executive-summary)
- **Vietnam:**
  - SEforALL Energizing Finance brief (c. 2021): ~51% of the population cook with Tier-4 clean fuels and ~22% still rely on traditional biomass stoves (Tier 0–1) — [SEforALL Vietnam brief](https://www.seforall.org/system/files/2021-10/TTP-Vietnam-Brief.pdf)
  - A clean-cooking roadmap puts access at 73.1%, with more than a quarter still on polluting fuels in 2019 — [SEforALL brief](https://www.seforall.org/system/files/2021-10/TTP-Vietnam-Brief.pdf)
  - The World Bank series shows 66.9% clean access in 2016 — [CEIC](https://www.ceicdata.com/en/vietnam/energy-production-and-consumption)
  - SoGA notes Vietnam has reduced reliance on solid fuels since 2010 — [SoGA Asia](https://www.stateofglobalair.org/resources/asia)
  - More than 2 million people in Vietnam reportedly still cook with beehive coal briquettes (undated) — [J. Health Res.](https://he01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/jhealthres/article/download/92845/72687/230749)
- **Market size (low reliability):**
  - "Fuel clean cookstoves market": USD 4.5 bn (2024), projected USD 9.2 bn by 2034 (7.5% CAGR). Methodology is gated — [Reports and Data](https://www.reportsanddata.com/report-detail/fuel-clean-cookstoves-market)
  - No figure was found for the biomass-gasifier-stove segment. Industrial "biomass gasification" market figures (USD 110–126 bn in 2024; Kings Research, IMARC, P&S) cover power, heat and fuel plants and should not be used as a stove proxy — [P&S Intelligence](https://www.psmarketresearch.com/market-analysis/biomass-gasification-market); [IMARC](https://imarcgroup.com/biomass-gasification-plant)
- **Enterprise finance and revenue (Clean Cooking Alliance):**
  - 2023 data (Industry Snapshot, Jul 2025):
    - Capital investment reached USD 218 m, 5x since 2018 and debt-driven; equity was at a five-year low.
    - Enterprise revenues were USD 166.7 m (+79% y/y), with 61% from product and fuel sales and 35% from carbon credits.
    - Carbon credit issuances rose 168%, contributing to a ~60% drop in average prices since early 2022 (oversupply).
    — [CCA news](https://cleancooking.org/news/new-report-clean-cooking-sector-reaches-inflection-point-amid-record-growth-and-rising-carbon-revenues/)
  - 2024 data (6th Snapshot, Jun 2026):
    - Total investment fell 10% and sector revenues fell 16%, the first decline in six years. Equity overtook debt.
    - Companies with carbon programs were more resilient and captured **95% of all 2024 investment**.
    — [CCA news](https://cleancooking.org/news/latest-clean-cooking-industry-snapshot-identifies-trends-in-investment-revenue-and-carbon-markets/)
  - The CCA website gives USD 196 m (2024) in tracked debt, equity and grants, against USD 8 bn/yr needed for universal access by 2030 — [CCA](https://cleancooking.org/)
- **IEA Summit on Clean Cooking in Africa (Paris, May 2024):** USD 2.2 bn in pledges — [IEA](https://www.iea.org/news/landmark-summit-mobilises-22-billion-to-make-2024-a-turning-point-for-clean-cooking-access-in-africa)
  - >USD 470 m disbursed by July 2025; a later status report says USD 740 m deployed across 22 African countries — [IEA](https://www.iea.org/news/new-commitments-to-clean-cooking-in-africa-reach-900-million-ahead-of-2nd-major-summit)
  - USD 900 m in new commitments came ahead of the 2nd Summit, held in Nairobi on 9–10 July 2026 — [IEA](https://www.iea.org/news/kenya-norway-us-and-iea-to-hold-2nd-major-summit-on-clean-cooking-in-africa)
  - CCA is being integrated into the IEA — [IEA](https://www.iea.org/news/clean-cooking-alliance-to-integrate-into-the-iea-in-major-step-forward-for-international-cooperation-on-clean-cooking-access)

### Inferences
- Vietnam household TAM, a rough estimate:
  - 22% Tier 0–1 × ~101 m people ≈ 22 m people, or ~5.5–6.5 m households (assuming ~3.5 persons per household). Population and household size are not sourced here.
  - At GaSIO's USD 25–60 price, a one-time replacement value is ≈ USD 140–390 m.
  - Using the broader "polluting fuels" definition (~25–30%) raises this by ~20–35%.
- SE Asia household TAM: 120 m people ≈ ~28–32 m households ≈ USD 0.7–1.9 bn one-time.
- Global household TAM: 2.1 bn people ≈ ~450–500 m households ≈ USD 11–30 bn one-time. Most of that is Sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia, where carbon-financed distributors (BURN, Koko, Envirofit and others) dominate.
- Without carbon finance, a USD 25–60 stove competes against near-zero-cost traditional stoves and against subsidised or cheap LPG and induction in Vietnam. CCA data shows that carbon revenue is now the main thing separating companies that survive from those that do not.

### Gaps
- No current (2023/2024) WHO GHO value for Vietnam's urban/rural clean-cooking share was retrievable. The figure is needed for a precise household count; the WHO cooking-fuel database is the primary source — [WHO GHO](https://www.who.int/data/gho/data/themes/air-pollution/cooking-fuel-and-technology-database-by-fuel-category)
- No credible market size for biomass gasifier (TLUD/forced-draft) cookstoves specifically.

## Q2. Industrial and agri-processing heat in Vietnam, and energy policy (PDP8, JETP, Net Zero)

### Takeaway
Vietnam's tea sector is mid-sized (~128,000 ha; ~137,000 t exported in 2025, worth ~USD 238 m) and fragmented: Thai Nguyen alone has ~67,500 tea households. That makes it a natural beach-head for 75 kW-scale heat units, and CCS has already worked in it under the EU SWITCH-Asia BEST project. The boiler retrofit market is larger: ~9,000 industrial boilers, of which an official estimate says >6,000 could be retrofitted or replaced, concentrated in textiles and footwear under export-buyer decarbonisation pressure. Policy is supportive but focused on power:
- PDP8 (revised April 2025) targets 1,523–2,699 MW of biomass power by 2030.
- JETP (USD 15.5 bn) is mostly for grid and power.
- Net Zero 2050 and the 2025–2028 pilot ETS (power, steel, cement) do not yet directly price SME process heat.

### Cited Findings
- **Tea:**
  - 2025 exports were ~136,952 t worth ~USD 238 m (−7.1% volume, −7.2% value y/y), at an average of ~USD 1,737/t. Pakistan took 30.1% of volume — [MAE (Ministry of Agriculture and Environment)](https://en.mae.gov.vn/quality-processing-and-market-development/vietnamese-tea-industry-establishes-a-new-global-standing-9183.htm)
  - Exports for 10M 2025 earned USD 189 m — [ITPC HCMC](https://itpc.hochiminhcity.gov.vn/web/en/-/vietnam-s-tea-exports-earn-189-mln-in-10m)
  - Planted area is ~128,000 ha (Feb 2026). Decision 431 targets 120,000–125,000 ha and 1.2–1.4 Mt of fresh leaf by 2030 — [MAE](https://en.mae.gov.vn/quality-processing-and-market-development/vietnamese-tea-industry-establishes-a-new-global-standing-9183.htm)
  - January 2026 exports were 41,000 t — [VnEconomy](https://en.vneconomy.vn/vietnam-exports-41000-tons-of-tea-in-january.htm)

- **CCS / VCBG track record (SWITCH-Asia BEST):**

  - Vietnam is party to the Global Methane Pledge (−30% CH4 vs 2020 by 2030) — [VnEconomy](https://vneconomy.vn/danh-gia-ndc-nam-2022-cap-nhat-cac-muc-tieu-giam-phat-thai-cho-giai-doan-moi-den-2035.htm)

### Inferences
- **Boiler retrofit SAM:** ~6,000 retrofittable boilers. At an assumed USD 20k–300k per burner retrofit (20 kg/h–20 t/h; this price range is an assumption, not sourced), that is roughly USD 0.1–1.8 bn. Textile and footwear exporters face buyer Scope-3 pressure (brands) and are the most motivated segment.
- **Agri-heat SAM:** The BEST target of 2,500 MSMEs in four northern provinces is a validated reference point. Nationally, tea, coffee drying, cinnamon/essential-oil distillation and cassava starch could plausibly be 10,000–30,000 small thermal users. This is unsourced; a census is a gap.
- **Fuel-price competition:** Pellet export demand (Japan/Korea biomass co-firing) is lifting Vietnamese residue prices toward export parity (~USD 130–160/t). That favours a gasifier that can run on low-grade residues (husk, straw, tea prunings, cinnamon bark) over pellet boilers.
- **Policy is a tailwind for power, not heat:** none of PDP8, JETP or the pilot ETS directly subsidises SME process heat. The main policy levers for GaSIO are carbon credits (Q4–Q6), green-buyer requirements, and air-quality enforcement on open straw burning.

### Gaps
- Number of tea processors and their fuel use (firewood t/yr, coal) nationally. VITAS/Vietnam Tea Association data was not reachable.
- Cinnamon, star anise and essential-oil distillery counts; cassava starch factory counts; coffee drying energy.
- Official coal consumption by industrial boilers (t/yr); SME boiler market value (USD).
- Whether the JETP Resource Mobilisation Plan includes any industrial-heat or biomass-thermal window.

## Q3. Biomass residue volumes in Vietnam

### Takeaway
Credible national figures put crop residues at ~79 Mt (MOIT 2017) to 95–98 Mt (IAE 2019). Rice straw (~45–54 Mt) and rice husk (~9–10 Mt) dominate, followed by bagasse (~5–6 Mt) and wood by-products/sawdust (~5 Mt). Most rice straw in the Mekong Delta is still burned in the field. The supply side is not a constraint; logistics and competing uses (pellet exports) are.

### Cited Findings
- MOIT-based breakdown: rice straw 44.97 Mt, rice husk 8.99 Mt, bagasse 5.96 Mt; wood by-products 5.35 Mt, of which 4.46 Mt is wood by-products and 0.89 Mt sawdust — [b-company.jp](https://b-company.jp/agricultural-byproducts-in-vietnam-industry-overview-and-business-opportunities/)

### Inferences
- Using MOIT figures, rice straw (~45 Mt) × an ~80% burn rate ≈ ~36 Mt burned per year, plus some husk and other residues dumped in rivers. This makes "~65 Mt burned or discarded" an upper-end estimate, plausible only with the higher straw estimates (54–97 Mt).
- Rice husk (~9 Mt) and sawdust are the best feedstocks for biochar and heat, because they are concentrated at mills. Straw is dispersed and seasonal; it suits household stoves and small dryers only near the source.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2025 national residue inventory (GIZ/MOIT "Climate Protection through Bioenergy Markets" outputs) was retrievable.
- Forestry harvest residues, and cassava, corn and cinnamon residue volumes, were not quantified.

## Q4. Vietnam carbon market: Decrees 06/2022, 119/2025, 29/2026, 112/2026, pilot ETS and Article 6

### Takeaway
Vietnam now has a full legal stack:
- **Decree 06/2022** is the base framework.
- **Decree 119/2025** (effective Aug 2025) sets up the pilot ETS for 2025–2028. It covers thermal power, steel and cement (~150 facilities), with free intensity-based allocation, offsets up to 30%, and auctioning from 2029.
- **Decree 29/2026** creates the carbon exchange under the Vietnam Stock Exchange (VNX).
- **Decree 112/2026** (effective 19 May 2026) governs international transfers and corresponding adjustments, with export caps of 50–90%.

On Article 6, there is a Singapore implementation agreement (signed Sep 2025, approved by the government in Aug 2026) and a JCM upgraded to Article 6 in May 2026. Trading on the exchange had not started as of 2025–early 2026 reports. For GaSIO, the realistic near-term route is Verra, Gold Standard or Puro credits under Decree 112 export rules, with a later option to sell offsets to ETS entities.

## Q5. Cookstove carbon credits: prices, integrity, CCP and CORSIA

### Takeaway
Cookstove credits split into two tiers.
- **Legacy credits:** ~USD 4/t on average (2026).
- **CORSIA Phase 1-eligible or CCP-labelled credits:** ~USD 10–18/t in 2026, with CCP Sub-Saharan Africa at ~USD 15 and top-integrity estimates up to ~USD 39.

The 2024 Berkeley study found 9.2x over-crediting. ICVCM (March 2025) then approved only three methodologies (GS Metered & Measured, GS TPDDTEC v4, Verra VM0050), with strict fNRB, charcoal and fuel-use conditions. A new entrant can earn premium prices only by using metered or measured monitoring and conservative fNRB (MoFuSS or the 0.3 default). This sharply cuts credits per stove, especially for Vietnam: project-reported fNRB was 67% versus a MoFuSS estimate of ~34%.

### Cited Findings

Gold Standard's metered methodology was closest to their estimates (1.5x). The drivers were exaggerated adoption and use, ignored stove stacking, and inflated fNRB — [Berkeley News](https://news.berkeley.edu/2024/01/23/as-carbon-offsets-cookstove-emission-credits-are-greatly-overestimated); [paper PDF](https://rael.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Gill-Wiehl-Kammen-Haya-Cooking-the-Books-Nature-Sustiainability-2024.pdf)

  - Abatable (Apr 2025): a "floor price" for high-quality cookstove credits of ~USD 15, rising to ~USD 39 for top-integrity CCP — [Abatable](https://abatable.com/blog/a-new-era-for-high-quality-carbon-avoidance-the-new-floor-price-for-cookstove-carbon-credits/)

### Inferences
- **For GaSIO stoves:** using VM0050 or GS Metered with a Vietnam fNRB of ~0.3 instead of ~0.67 roughly halves credits per stove versus legacy Vietnamese projects. At USD 10–15/t, plausible revenue is ~USD 10–40 per stove per year, assuming ~1–3 tCO2e/stove/yr; this per-stove range is an unsourced assumption.
  - Even so, carbon revenue could subsidise much of a USD 25–60 stove within 1–3 years, if usage is metered (IoT temperature or fuel sensors), which adds cost.
- **Biochar co-product:** GaSIO's distinctive angle is a stove that makes char. That could earn an additional removal credit under CSI Global Artisan C-Sink or similar (Q6) at ~€105/t CO2 for artisanal char, about 7–10x the avoidance credit price. This "stacking" avoids the fNRB problem entirely.
  - Stacking must avoid double-claiming: char-burning credits and char-sequestration credits are not both allowed under some rules (not verified).
- **CORSIA vs CCP trade-off:** CORSIA requires a host-country Letter of Authorization and a corresponding adjustment, which in Vietnam means Decree 112 approval and a possible cap. CCP does not require a CA, but its premium is smaller.

### Gaps
- No Asia-specific (as opposed to Sub-Saharan Africa) CCP cookstove price assessment was found.
- No Gold Standard-registered Vietnam cookstove project was identified.
- Typical tCO2e/stove/yr for TLUD/gasifier stoves under VM0050 with Vietnam fNRB was not found.

## Q6. Biochar carbon removal (CDR) market

### Takeaway
Biochar is the volume leader of durable CDR:
- ~3.04 Mt contracted from 2022 to H1 2025 (1.6 Mt in H1 2025 alone) and ~658 kt delivered.
- Market value grew from USD 14.6 m (2022) to USD 181.5 m (2024).
- Prices are ~€120–130/t (Puro CORCCHAR index, 2026) and ~USD 115–177/t across sources. Artisanal Indian char is ~€105/t.

  - **Puro.earth Biochar Methodology, Edition 2025:** stricter biomass sourcing, chain-of-custody and pyrolysis-gas control; version labels conflict across sources — [EDF comparison](https://www.edf.org/comparison-biochar-carbon-market-protocols); [Carbon Unity guide](https://carbonunity.earth/guides/which-carbon-standard)
  - **Verra VM0044 v1.2:** active 27 Jun 2025, adds an investment-analysis additionality step, CCP-approved 2025 — [Verra minor revision](https://verra.org/verra-publishes-minor-revision-to-biochar-methodology/)
    - The major revision v2.0 was under consultation until 17 Aug 2026, covering H/Corg and moisture monitoring and transport emissions, and will go to ICVCM — [Verra consultation](https://verra.org/consultation-major-revision-to-biochar-methodology-vm0044/); [Quantum Commodity Intelligence](https://www.qcintel.com/carbon/article/verra-plans-biochar-methodology-update-new-version-in-2026-50655.html)
  - EBC/Puro thresholds: H/Corg < 0.7 and O/C < 0.4 (EBC) — [Stasis Carbon](https://www.stasiscarbon.com/blog/biochar-permanence.html)

### Inferences
- **Household-stove char:** a GaSIO stove producing ~0.3–0.5 kg char per cook-day, about 100–180 kg/yr per household, is an assumption that needs company data. At ~1.5–2.5 tCO2e/t net, that is ~0.15–0.45 tCO2e/household/yr. At ~€105/t, that is ~€15–45/household/yr, in the same range as or above avoidance credits.
  - It requires village-level aggregation (>50 families), char collection or soil application, and digital MRV under Global Artisan C-Sink.
- **Agri-heat units and boiler retrofits:** units of 75 kW–3 MW producing char at mills are better suited to Puro, Isometric or CSI industrial standards at €120–150/t. Microsoft-type buyers want large, concentrated, auditable volumes, as in the Varaha/India model.
- The biochar removal market (~USD 180 m in 2024) is small but growing ~5x/yr, and is dominated by a handful of buyers. Concentration risk is high: Microsoft pausing or slowing would reprice the market.

### Gaps
- No biochar removal projects registered in Vietnam on Puro, Isometric or CSI were found (not searched exhaustively).
- CDR.fyi's own 2025/2026 average price per delivered tonne was not visible.
- The Global Artisan C-Sink price for cookstove-derived char was not found; only the general artisanal figure of ~€105.

## Q9. Synthesised TAM / SAM / SOM picture (for the report writer)

### Takeaway
Treat these as indicative order-of-magnitude figures built from the cited inputs above plus clearly flagged assumptions. Every segment except boiler retrofits is a few hundred million USD of equipment value in Vietnam. Carbon credits (avoidance plus removal) can roughly double unit economics per stove or unit. The binding constraints are MRV cost, Decree 112 export rules and buyer concentration, not market size.

### Cited Findings
- Inputs, all cited above:
  - Vietnam ~22% Tier 0–1 biomass cooking (c. 2021) — [SEforALL](https://www.seforall.org/system/files/2021-10/TTP-Vietnam-Brief.pdf)
  - SE Asia 120 m without clean cooking (2026) — [IEA SEAO 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/southeast-asia-energy-outlook-2026/executive-summary)
  - Global 2.1 bn (2023) — [Tracking SDG7 2025](https://www.irena.org/Publications/2025/Jun/Tracking-SDG-7-The-Energy-Progress-Report-2025)
  - ~9,000 boilers, >6,000 retrofittable — [NGO Environment](https://ngoenvironment.com/en/reducing-emissions-through-industrial-boiler-conversion.html)
  - Tea ~128,000 ha, 67,500 Thai Nguyen households — [MAE](https://en.mae.gov.vn/quality-processing-and-market-development/vietnamese-tea-industry-establishes-a-new-global-standing-9183.htm); [Vietnam.vn](https://www.vietnam.vn/en/mo-rong-thi-truong-tieu-thu-bang-san-xuat-sach)

  - Biochar removal market USD 181.5 m (2024) — [CDR.fyi](https://www.cdr.fyi/blog/biochar-carbon-removal-market-snapshot-2025)

- **Tailwinds:**
  - The ETS pilot, carbon exchange and Article 6 legal clarity (Decrees 119/2025, 29/2026, 112/2026).

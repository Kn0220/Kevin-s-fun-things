## 2. Prior art: continuous-feed gasifier stoves and what (if anything) is distinct about "volumetric continuous"

### Takeaway
Continuous feeding of small-particle biomass in household and institutional gasifier stoves is well-established prior art going back at least to Belonio's continuous-flow rice husk gasifiers (Philippines, 2000s–2012). Chinese patents also cover household semi-gasifiers and feeder-equipped biomass gasification furnaces. Novelty therefore cannot rest on "continuous feeding", "negative-pressure reactor", "syngas plus biochar", or "multi-feedstock". The brochure suggests "volumetric" refers to a maximized thermal-power-to-reactor-volume ratio, which is a performance attribute and not a distinct gasification regime. Any defensible novelty has to sit in specific mechanical features (feed geometry, air staging, char extraction), which I could not see because the claims are unavailable.

### Cited Findings
- **CCS's own description (primary document, read in full).** The VCBG brochure lists these advantages: (1) "gasification is not interrupted because feed is continuous; minimalist design"; (2) "maximum ratio of thermal power to equipment volume" (Tỷ lệ công suất nhiệt / thể tích thiết bị tối đa); (3) "reaction chamber under negative pressure, preventing toxic gas leaks"; (4) "low fuel requirements, reducing fuel-processing cost". It calls VCBG "the most advanced gasification technology version available today". The brochure gives no mechanism detail such as reactor geometry, air path, or char discharge. — [Oxfam/CCS brochure "Công nghệ khí hóa sinh khối liên tục theo thể tích – Những điều cần biết" (PDF)](https://oi-files-cng-v2-prod.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/vietnam.oxfam.org/s3fs-public/file_attachments/Brochure%20VCBG%20-%20final.pdf)
- **Belonio, continuous-flow rice husk gas stoves.** The CLSU (Central Luzon State University) paper "Design and Performance of a Household-Size Continuous-Flow Rice Husk Gas Stove" describes them. — [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/6463495/DESIGN_AND_PERFORMANCE_OF_A_HOUSEHOLD_SIZE_CONTINUOUS_FLOW_RICE_HUSK_GAS_STOVE_1_INTRODUCTION). The stove can "be operated continuously until all cooking jobs is finished". — [bioenergylists node 2962](https://stoves.bioenergylists.org/node/2962)
- **Belonio and Bhuiyan (ICDRET 2012).** "Design of a continuous-type rice husk gasifier stove and power generation device for Bangladesh household" reports about 1.1 kg/h fuel use for the downdraft variant. — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Title:-Design-of-a-continuous-%E2%80%94-Type-rice-husk-and-Belonio-Bhuiyan/5559a8e178ec6ffb62084aa99a66c2852b2c09d1); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/261123091_Title_Design_of_a_continuous_-_Type_rice_husk_gasifier_stove_and_power_generation_device_for_Bangladesh_household)
- **Belonio, continuous-flow rice husk gasifier for thermal applications.** This is an institutional-scale continuous design. — [bioenergylists node 2878](https://stoves.bioenergylists.org/node/2878). CRHET also catalogues continuous-type rice husk gasifier stove models 10D, 12D and 14D alongside batch types. — [bioenergylists taxonomy: Rice Husk Gasifier](https://stoves.bioenergylists.org/taxonomy/term/1632)
- **Belonio rice husk gas stove handbook.** Describes a top-lit updraft (TLUD) column of rice husk, presented as the first TLUD able to burn small-particle fuel, with rice husk "continuously fed in the reactor". — [Belonio Rice Husk Gas Stove Handbook (PDF)](http://www.bioenergylists.org/stovesdoc/Belonio/Belonio_gasifier.pdf)
- **Energypedia/GIZ micro-gasification.** Describes natural-draft continuous-feed rice husk stoves with conical hoppers as "quasi" or "semi" gasifiers, distinct from batch-loaded fan TLUDs. — [Energypedia: Gasifier Stoves](https://energypedia.info/wiki/Gasifier_Stoves); [GIZ Micro-gasification: Cooking with gas from biomass (PDF)](https://energypedia.info/images/f/f6/Micro_Gasification_Cooking_with_gas_from_biomass.pdf)

- **Chinese prior art.** CN105482853B "生物质气化炉" claims a biomass gasification furnace with a feeding device connected in series to a gasifier, separation/cooling and burner, plus an automatic lid. — [CN105482853B](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN105482853B/zh). CN102538019B "一种户用生物质半气化炉" is a household biomass semi-gasifier stove. — [CN102538019B](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN102538019B/zh). CN101412915B is a continuous pyrolysis-gasification furnace for steady gas supply (industrial). — [CN101412915B](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN101412915B/zh). - **Chinese literature on tobacco-curing gasifiers.** Notes that continuous large-volume gas supply and "adding fuel without extinguishing" were achieved, while automatic fuel drop and tar remained unsolved. This shows continuous refuelling is a known design goal in China. — [China Agricultural Science Bulletin 2010 (casb.org.cn)](https://www.casb.org.cn/CN/Y2010/V26/I14/392)
- **Vietnamese prior art.** VINASILIC's "Vietnam Magic Flame" TLUD pellet stove (2010, with Belonio, Tran Binh and others), priced at US$10–15. — [bioenergylists node 2618](https://stoves.bioenergylists.org/node/2618). - **Mimi Moto (fan-assisted pellet gasifier).** It is the closest commercial performance benchmark. The Clean Cooking Catalog lists Tier 5 thermal efficiency (50%), Tier 5 CO and Tier 4 PM2.5, the same tier profile CCS claims for GaSIO. - **Other multifuel forced-draft gasifier stoves** on agricultural residues are documented in the peer-reviewed literature. — [Sustainability 2020, 3 kW air-forced multifuel gasification stove](https://doi.org/10.3390/su12187723); [Sustainability 2017, Comparing performance of biomass gasifier stoves (TU Delft-affiliated)](https://doi.org/10.3390/su9071140)

### Inferences
- **Not novel as concepts:** (a) continuous or semi-continuous feeding; (b) induced-draft or negative-pressure reactor (any fan-in-flue design); (c) syngas burned close-coupled plus biochar co-product; (d) multi-feedstock capability with residues like rice husk and sawdust; (e) the household-to-industrial scale range. Belonio's work alone anticipates continuous small-particle gasifier stoves at both household and thermal-application scale. Chinese utility models cover feeder-equipped household and semi-household gasifiers.
- **Where the edge could plausibly be:** a combination that keeps a TLUD-like clean-burn regime (low PM and CO typical of a well-controlled pyrolytic front) while refilling continuously and extracting char without disrupting the reaction zone, in a cheap, low-part-count build ("thiết kế tối giản"). Most continuous rice-husk "semi-gasifiers" historically struggled with smoke during refuelling and needed constant attention (Energypedia). If GaSIO really achieves Tier 4 PM2.5 with continuous refuelling on mixed residues, that is a meaningful engineering advance, but an incremental one: it is an inventive-step question, not a pioneering one.
- **"Volumetric"** appears to describe power density (thermal kW per reactor volume) according to CCS's own brochure, and Oxfam documents also use "Vertical". It is a brand term, not a technical class recognized in the gasification literature. I found no hits for the phrase outside CCS, Oxfam, EU and CTC-N material.

### Gaps
- No technical drawing or claim text was available, so I could not determine the actual mechanism (updraft vs downdraft vs cross-draft; whether char is discharged continuously, for example by grate or screw, or accumulates).
- I did not retrieve primary data on Oorja (First Energy, India) or the Philips HD4012/HD4008 fan stoves in this session. From general knowledge, both are batch-fed forced-draft designs, which makes them weaker prior art for continuous feeding but relevant for fan-assisted gasification.
- I did not retrieve Paul Anderson's specific continuous or semi-continuous TLUD (char-removal) publications.

## 4. Plausibility of performance claims

### Takeaway
The Tier 5 CO, Tier 4 PM2.5 profile is plausible for a fan-assisted gasifier: the commercial Mimi Moto has exactly this profile. The 83.7% "maximum thermal efficiency" for a household cooking stove is not credible as an ISO 19867-1 cooking thermal efficiency. The best lab-tested biomass gasifier stoves reach about 40–50%, and it most likely reflects a different metric or calculation basis. The 95% fixed-carbon biochar claim is only plausible on a dry, ash-free basis with woody feedstock, and is impossible on an as-received basis for rice-husk char. The 73–75% industrial heat efficiency is plausible for a close-coupled gasifier-burner. The 3 MW and 20 t/h boiler claims are unsupported by any public evidence.

### Cited Findings
- **Efficiency definition.** ISO 19867-1 defines thermal efficiency and separate char-related metrics (char energy efficiency, char mass productivity), and lists TLUDs as batch-loaded stoves. — [ISO 19867-1:2018 OBP text](https://www.iso.org/obp/ui/#iso:std:iso:19867:-1:ed-1:v1:en)
- **Why the char basis matters.** An OSTI paper on stove retrofits notes that WBT (Water Boiling Test) and historical lab reports state thermal efficiency "with char". TLUD-type stoves like the Mimi Moto produce char that may or may not be used for cooking. — [OSTI: Retrofitting stoves with forced jets](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/2418490)
- **How much energy stays in the char.** Tryner et al. (2014/2015) found up to 60% of input fuel energy can remain as char in natural-draft TLUD semi-gasifiers. The highest thermal efficiency they measured was 42%, and they recommend reporting both thermal and overall efficiency. — [Tryner et al., Envirofit-hosted PDF](https://envirofit.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/2015-The-effect-of-fuel-type-etc_Tryner.pdf); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/265387440_The_effects_of_fuel_type_and_stove_design_on_emissions_and_efficiency_of_natural-draft_semi-gasifier_biomass_cookstoves)
- **Literature range.** A systematic review reports gasifier cookstove thermal efficiencies typically in the 30–50% range. — [Thermal performance of gasifier cooking stoves: systematic review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/367009021_Thermal_performance_of_gasifier_cooking_stoves_A_systematic_literature_review)
- **Pellet gasifier bench results.** Pellet gasifier stoves fall between Tier 4 and Tier 5 on thermal efficiency on the bench, with one ISO 19867-1 pellet test at 49%. — [World Bioenergy Association: Pellet cookstoves (PDF)](https://www.worldbioenergy.org/uploads/Pellets%20a%20modern%20cooking%20solution.pdf). Mimi Moto: about 47–50%, Tier 5 thermal efficiency at 50%. — [CCA Catalog item](https://cleancooking.org/innovations/clean-burning-gasification-stove/)
- **CCS industrial figures.** The industrial gasifier was recognized by MOIT/DANIDA (2016) at about 75% efficiency — [ccspin.org](https://ccspin.org/index.php/vi/hoa-t-a-ng/ca-ng-ngha-i/267-cong-ngh-1). CCS's VCBG page states industrial efficiency up to about 73% — [ccs.org.vn VCBG page](https://ccs.org.vn/cong-nghe-va-san-pham/chi-tiet/cong-nghe-khi-hoa-sinh-khoi-lien-tuc-theo-the-tich-vcbg)
- **Biochar fixed carbon.** Fixed carbon in TLUD stove char is about 86.2 ± 2.4% (volatile matter about 12%, ash about 1.9%, HHV about 32.3 MJ/kg) in a Tanzanian natural-draft TLUD study — [Misginna (Makerere-hosted PDF)](https://news.mak.ac.ug/wp-content/uploads/2011/06/Misginna.pdf). TLUD coconut-shell char was 64.8% fixed carbon (top) and 45.9% (bottom) — [Curay et al., VSU ATR](https://atr.vsu.edu.ph/article/view/90). TLUD biosolids char had much lower fixed carbon than furnace char — [AGRIS record](https://agris.fao.org/search/ar/records/65df0cc54c5aef494fdf4567)
- **Cost claims.** The brochure claims savings of up to 50% vs coal and 80% vs LPG or oil. The investment is 3.2–8.2 million VND per unit for micro-enterprises and households (2–3 t produce per year), and 130–270 million VND for small enterprises (80–100 t per year). — [Oxfam/CCS VCBG brochure (PDF)](https://oi-files-cng-v2-prod.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/vietnam.oxfam.org/s3fs-public/file_attachments/Brochure%20VCBG%20-%20final.pdf)
- **CCS boiler history.** CCS reports converting boiler heat supply to bamboo-sawdust gasification (2010), rice-husk gasification (2011), and applications for small-capacity boilers (2013). This is from a search summary of the CCS VCBG page. — [ccs.org.vn VCBG page](https://ccs.org.vn/cong-nghe-va-san-pham/chi-tiet/cong-nghe-khi-hoa-sinh-khoi-lien-tuc-theo-the-tich-vcbg)

### Inferences
- **83.7% household efficiency.** This would be about 1.7 times the best ISO-tested biomass gasifier stoves and above typical LPG stove efficiencies (general knowledge: about 50–65%). Likely explanations:
  - (a) "thermal efficiency with char credit" on a test where much fuel energy stayed in the char;
  - (b) a gasifier cold-gas or hot-gas efficiency (syngas energy over fuel energy), not heat delivered to the pot;
  - (c) a non-standard vessel or heat exchanger, or a single best test run ("maximum").
  
  The Tier 5 claim only needs ≥50% (consistent with the Catalog's Tier 5 at 50% for Mimi Moto), so Tier 5 is plausible even if 83.7% is not an ISO cooking efficiency. Ask for the ISO 19867-1 report's thermal efficiency, with and without char, and its char energy efficiency.
- **95% fixed carbon.** This is achievable for well-carbonized woody char on a dry, ash-free (daf) basis at high final temperature. It exceeds the 46–86% range reported for TLUD chars on typical bases. Rice-husk char typically carries very high ash (general knowledge: often 40%+ of the char), so 95% fixed carbon cannot hold for rice-husk char on a dry basis. The claim needs a stated basis, feedstock, and an accredited proximate or ultimate analysis (ideally EBC or IBI parameters: organic carbon, H/Corg, PAHs).
- **Tier 4 PM2.5 and Tier 5 CO.** This is credible and a genuinely good result for a residue-burning stove. It matches the best-in-class fan gasifier (Mimi Moto). The open question is whether it holds during continuous refuelling and on loose residues rather than pellets.
- **Industrial 73–75%.** This is plausible as a heat-to-process figure for a close-coupled gasifier and burner on a dryer, roaster or boiler, where flue losses dominate. It is not independently verified beyond the MOIT/DANIDA recognition.
- **3 MW and 20 t/h steam retrofits.** 20 t/h of saturated steam is roughly 13–15 MW of thermal output (general engineering estimate). Note the internal mismatch: "industrial up to 3 MW" alongside "boiler retrofits up to 20 t/h" implies either multiple gasifier modules or a claim beyond demonstrated scale. Public evidence only shows small-boiler conversions (2010–2013) and tea-roasting units. Treat large-boiler claims as design capability, not proven deployment.

### Gaps
- Not found: the actual ISO 19867-1 data sheet (metrics, fuel, char treatment), the biochar lab analysis, or any third-party measurement of the industrial units.
- Not found: the CCS boiler reference list with capacities (t/h), operating hours, and customer names.

## 5. TRL assessment per product line (NASA/EU 1–9) and what is needed to defend novelty

### Takeaway
The household stove and the tea-roasting gasifier are near-commercial (TRL 7–8), with field deployment backed by donors since 2017. Industrial tea heat and small-boiler retrofits are at TRL 6–7. Large boiler retrofits (up to 20 t/h) and the 3 kW battery-fan camping stove have no public evidence beyond claims (TRL 4–5). The IP position is thin and territorial. Defending novelty requires obtaining and reading the actual claims, a freedom-to-operate (FTO) and novelty search against the Belonio and Chinese prior art, independent replicated testing, and a decision on foreign filing (PCT priority is probably already lost).

### Cited Findings
- **Tea roasting/drying.** Piloted in Định Hóa, Thái Nguyên since 2017 (2018–2019 pilot) — [SWITCH-Asia BEST](https://www.switch-asia.eu/project/biomass-gasification-technology/); [Oxfam: When waste reduces pollution](https://vietnam.oxfam.org/latest/stories/when-waste-reduces-environmental-pollution-story-dinh-hoa-thai-nguyen). What would be needed to defend or strengthen novelty and the technology edge:
1. **Obtain the full certificates and claim text** for VN invention 2023, VN utility solution 2017, the DK 2020 right and the two pending VN applications. Confirm the applicant (CCS vs EBG vs the individual inventor), status and fee payments. If any is a Danish utility model, request a DKPTO examination to test validity before relying on it.
2. **Run a professional novelty and FTO search** with CPC/IPC codes for gasifier stoves and gasifiers with feeding and char discharge (for example F24B, F24C, C10J 3/20–3/34, C10B). Cover CN utility models (very dense prior art), PH (Belonio/CLSU), IN, US, and the CCS's own pre-filing publications (2015 award, workshops, press).
3. **Decide on international filing.** If no PCT was filed within 12 months of the earliest priority date, foreign protection for the original embodiment is likely unavailable. New improvements (for example the camping stove or the tea-roasting device) could still go through a PCT within 12 months of their VN filings, but only if they have not been published first.
4. **Get independent, replicated testing.** Publish the ISO 19867-1 report in the Clean Cooking Catalog (efficiency with and without char, char energy efficiency, PM2.5 and CO with continuous refuelling, on at least two residue fuels). Add a second lab (for example Aprovecho or a CCA testing center in Asia), accredited biochar analysis (EBC or IBI), and field measurements or monitoring for one industrial unit (stack emissions and heat balance).
5. **Treat trade secrets and know-how** (dimensions, air-staging ratios, fabrication jigs, the local manufacturer training curriculum) as the more realistic moat. The patents are territorial and likely narrow, and local fabricators were trained under BEST.
